# Project: Stavební deník (construction site diary)

## Architecture (current stack)

- **Backend**: Kotlin 2.4, Ktor 3.6, jOOQ 3.21, Flyway 12, **PostgreSQL 18 only** (schema uses `uuidv7()`), Argon2id passwords, JWT in an HttpOnly cookie.
- **Frontend**: Vite 8, React 19, React Router 7, Tailwind CSS 4 (`frontend/`, its own pnpm workspace; `frontend/pnpm-lock.yaml` is committed and CI and the image build install with `--frozen-lockfile`, so a `package.json` that disagrees with it fails the build).
- **PDF export** (`GET /api/reports/{id}/pdf` by report id, `GET /api/projects/{projectId}/reports/{idOrDate}/pdf` by id or by day inside one project; a bare date on the first route is `400`, because dates repeat across projects and an entry of another project must never be exported by accident; an unknown entry is `404`):
  - The `typst` command-line tool (`0.15.1` in the Docker image and CI, pinned by SHA-256 checksum) renders the fixed template `backend/src/main/resources/pdf/report.typ`.
  - User text never becomes part of the template: it travels as JSON data (`data.json`) and is shown as plain text, so it cannot be executed as typst code.
  - Each export gets its own temporary directory, a 20 s timeout (the process is killed), and at most two exports run at the same time. A third waits for a free slot for at most 10 s and is then refused with `503`, `Retry-After: 10` and `code: PDF_BUSY`, so a burst of exports cannot hold request threads and browsers for minutes. A person may start at most **20 exports per 5 minutes** (`429` with `Retry-After`; an attempt answered `PDF_BUSY` or the `503` without typst is given back, any other counts).
  - Without typst the endpoint answers `503` too (without that code); it never returns an empty PDF. The project page offers a PDF button for **every** entry in the list, not only the newest.
  - **What the document says** (backlog B8; the data is `ReportPdfData`, one section per builder function in `PdfExportService`):
    - Order: the title, **part A** (the identification of the diary: the project, the site manager, the author's supervision, the persons with a role and the scope of their authorisation and responsibility, the authorised persons with their function, the subcontractors and the supporting documents; from signature format 3 as it stood on the day of signing, see "Part A" below), **part B** (the entry), the signature with "Konec denního záznamu č. N", and then what was added after the signature (acknowledgements, addenda, entries of other parties, marked as not part of the signature; on a draft, which has no signature, an entry of another party stands under a neutral heading).
    - Part B prints the **content** the signature covers: the number, the author, the late-entry flag and reason, the control-day flag, the construction object, the weather, the workers with a total, the work description, the nine fields of `EntryDetails` and the photos (each with the SHA-256 of its file; the images themselves are not in the PDF). **Every heading is printed**, with "bez záznamu" where nothing was entered. Not everything the signature covers is printed: for a photo the id, the SHA-256 of the thumbnail and the uploader's id, and the project's id (the project is named by its name). And not everything printed is covered: the time a photo was uploaded, and the names of the author and the uploader (read from the users as they are now, backlog B9). The signer's name and ČKAIT number are covered and printed **as stored at signing** from format 3 on (before, they are read from the account as it is now). A very long name or ČKAIT number of the signer is cut with an ellipsis in the signature block (the signature check shows it in full).
    - A signed entry whose hash or audit row is missing says so in words: in signature format 1 as an old entry (before hashes, or before the audit trail in test data), from format 2 on as a failure ("záznam nelze ověřit"), like `ReportSignature.check`. So does a format 3 entry that lost its part A snapshot or its signer: the page then prints none of part A (no rows, no persons) and no signer, and says so; only the title, the footer and the first two rows of part A keep the project's name and address as they are now, which the note under part A says (`PdfExportService.PART_A_MISSING_NOTE`).
    - The signature block (signer, time, entry id, the SHA-256 of the content, from format 3 the SHA-256 of the part A snapshot, and the SHA-256 of the entry's `report.lock` row in the audit chain) is never split between two pages and is followed by "Konec denního záznamu č. N", the electronic form of striking out blank space (a draft says in words that it has no number yet). The audit row is printed so that the entry is tied to the chain (its `after` snapshot holds the content hash); **the head of the chain is not printed**, because it changes with every action and tells every reader how busy the whole company is. An entry with no `report.lock` row (signed before the audit trail existed, test data only) says so instead of failing.
    - **Text without a space** (a 500-character scope or function typed or pasted without one) would run over the edge of its table cell and of the page. In the tables of persons (the managers, the supervision and the authorised persons) a word of more than 16 characters (7 in the narrow ČKAIT column, which is 3.2 cm wide, so that a usual number is never broken) is given a place to break after every 16 (7), a zero-width space that prints nothing; it is in the text that is copied out of the PDF, which is why the length is not shorter. `RealTypstPdfTest` prints 500 characters without a space in each cell and checks that every one is there and that no text reaches past the margin. The other tables and rows of part A do not do this yet.
    - A draft carries "KONCEPT – NEPODEPSÁNO" across every page. Every page has a footer: project, number (or "bez čísla"), date and "Strana X z Y".
    - Dates are Czech (`28. 9. 2026`, with the weekday for the day of the entry) and times are **Prague time with the zone** (`14:32:07 SELČ (UTC+2)`, `CzechFormat`), also the day the identification is "valid on" (decision D10). The API and the audit snapshots keep ISO dates and UTC.
- **Docker image** (`Dockerfile`):
  - **Base image:** Ktor + the built SPA + typst on a **glibc** (Ubuntu) Temurin JRE. It must not be Alpine: the password library `argon2-jvm` loads a native library through JNA, and on Alpine (musl libc) the JVM dies with SIGSEGV as soon as a password is hashed (creating a user). The application also hashes and verifies one throwaway password at startup (`PasswordService.ensureWorks`), so such a broken environment fails at deploy time, not at the first account creation.
  - **The application does not run as root:** the entrypoint (`/app/entrypoint.sh`, written by the Dockerfile) starts as root only to give the data volume (`/data/uploads`, root-owned when Fly mounts it) to the user `app`, then drops privileges with `setpriv` before the JVM starts; the CI boot test checks the process user and the owner of the photo directory.
  - **Memory:** `JAVA_TOOL_OPTIONS` caps the heap at 40 % of the machine (`-XX:MaxRAMPercentage=40`) and ends the process on an out-of-memory error (Fly restarts it); the rest is for what the heap does not hold: Argon2 (up to 4 x 64 MiB native), image decoding, class metadata, threads and the typst processes. `fly.toml` asks for a `shared-cpu-1x` machine with **1 GB** (Fly's default is 256 MB); change that value together with the percentage.
- **Testing**:
  - Backend: JUnit 5, Testcontainers `postgres:18-alpine`, Ktor `testApplication`, WireMock / MockEngine. Tests use a fake typst; `RealTypstPdfTest` runs the real tool and is skipped when typst is not installed (CI installs it and sets `REQUIRE_TYPST=1`, which turns a missing typst into a failure).
  - Frontend: Vitest unit tests (`pnpm test` in `frontend/`, run by the lint/build job of CI): the plain-logic modules in `frontend/src/lib/*.test.ts` (the draft store, the draft sync, the form model, the Czech dates under several time zones, the logic of the entry as a document), `colorTokens.test.ts` (the colour tokens and the classes of the source against WCAG AA, see "The screens"), `components/ui/cn.test.ts` (class merging), `components/ui/Modal.test.tsx` and `components/EntryDocument.test.tsx` (the document and the signature card). A test file that needs a DOM starts with `// @vitest-environment jsdom` (jsdom and Testing Library are dev dependencies; jsdom has `<dialog>` but not `showModal()`, which the test supplies); the others run in node. What needs a real browser (focus, the backdrop, layout) is covered by the Playwright suite.
  - E2E: Playwright against the live stack (Ktor on :8080, Vite dev server on :5173 proxying `/api`), and in CI **also against the built production image** (`scripts/ci/e2e-image.sh`: image in `APP_ENV=production`, as the database role that owns nothing, schema migrated by the owner, the built SPA served by Ktor with its real security headers and secure cookies). The second run is what can see a broken production bundle, a Content Security Policy the SPA violates, a missing grant for the application's role or a file the non-root user cannot write.
  - The suite has three Playwright projects: `chromium` (desktop, every spec except `mobile-*.spec.ts`), `mobile` (a phone of 360 x 780 CSS px with touch) and `mobile-landscape` (the same phone held sideways, 780 x 360); the two phone projects run only `mobile-*.spec.ts` (`pnpm exec playwright test --project=mobile --project=mobile-landscape`). `e2e/mobile-pages.spec.ts` makes a project with long names and words that cannot be broken (63 to 120 letters), members, a handover, a draft, a late and a signed entry through the API, then visits every main page and requires that nothing scrolls sideways, that logout (top right) and the way into the pages are whole on the screen, that every button and one-line field is at least 44 px high and every field at least 16 px, and that the current tab is told apart by more than colour. A new page belongs in that spec. `e2e/entry-as-document.spec.ts` (desktop) covers the entry as a document, the lists and the project header (it was written first and fails against the page before backlog B4); `e2e/ui-foundations.spec.ts` (desktop) covers what a width and a pointer decide: a `className` that wins, the users' actions at 700 to 1400 px, a modal dialog's scroll lock, description and Escape, and when the new-password form calls a mistake a mistake.
- **Repository root**: only the Playwright suite (`e2e/`, `playwright.config.ts`, `scripts/dev/e2e-prepare.ts`) and the commit hooks (husky, lint-staged, prettier) live in the root `package.json`. The former Next.js + Prisma application was removed (2026-10-09); it is in the git history if anything is needed from it.

## jOOQ code generation

Generated classes live in `backend/src/generated/jooq` and are meant to be committed, so builds, tests and Docker/Fly builds never need a database.

```bash
./gradlew :backend:generateJooq   # starts postgres:18-alpine via Testcontainers, runs Flyway, generates Kotlin
```

Run it after every migration change and commit the result. CI job "jOOQ codegen drift check" regenerates the sources and fails the PR when they differ from what is committed (modified, added or deleted files); it uploads the fresh output as the `jooq-generated-sources` artifact.

To check for drift locally (needs Docker; empty output means in sync):

```bash
./gradlew :backend:generateJooq && git status --short backend/src/generated/jooq
```

If two branches change the schema, never merge the generated files by hand: resolve the migrations, then regenerate.

The schema lives in the Flyway migrations (`backend/src/main/resources/db/migration/V<n>__*.sql`). Nothing alters the schema at runtime.

**Until the first release the existing migrations are edited in place.** The project owner decided this on 2026-10-19: the service runs nowhere but in the tests and in GitHub Actions, so no database outside them exists, and every database there is built from the migrations from scratch. A change goes into the file that defines the object it concerns (the baseline `V1__initial_schema.sql` owns most tables, with their first triggers; a later file only for a trigger or a table of its own), and **no `V<n>` file is added for it**. It was done on 2026-10-08 (the former `V2` defaults were folded into `V1`, a report's date became a real `DATE`, `weather` became nullable, and the foreign keys from the diary's records to their project, report and author became `ON DELETE RESTRICT`: those to the user who signed are `ON DELETE SET NULL`, `project_members` still cascades; signed rows are protected by the triggers of V5 and V11) and on 2026-10-19 (hash version 2 of the audit log, backlog C3: `audit_log` in `V1` was redefined, a payload, a salt, a project and a version in place of before, after, ip and user agent, and `projects.id` became a random UUID; the triggers of V1, V2 and V12 are table level and did not change) and on 2026-10-22 (signature format 3, backlog C2: `daily_reports.signerName`, `signerCkaitNumber`, `partASnapshot` and `workStateChanges`, `projects.authorSupervisionName` and `authorSupervisionAuthorization`, `project_members.scope` in `V1`, and the signature-format checks of `V14`; the signed-records triggers of V5 and V11 compare whole rows and did not change) and again on 2026-10-22 (two-party signing of site handovers, backlog A3: the table `site_handover_signatures` in `V1`, its triggers and the new trigger of the protocol in `V5`, and `site_handovers.signedById` and `signedAt` are gone). **From the first `v*` tag every schema change is a new migration** (`V<n>`, the next number), because **the rule "a released migration never changes" starts with the first `v*` tag, and CI enforces it from then on** (job `immutable-migrations`, `scripts/ci/check-immutable-migrations.sh`): Flyway keeps a checksum of every migration it ran, so an edited one stops every database that already ran it. The script finds the newest `v*` tag reachable from `HEAD` and compares `backend/src/main/resources/db/migration/` with the tree at that tag: an added file passes, a file that was modified, renamed or deleted since the tag fails the job and is named. Until a `v*` tag exists the script says so and passes; in a shallow clone it fails instead of passing (the job checks out with `fetch-depth: 0`), and so does a tag at which that directory holds no migration (the directory moved: update the script). `db/grants/app-role.sql` is not guarded: it is not a versioned migration, and `migrate` applies it again at every release. The script has its own test (`check-immutable-migrations.test.sh`, throwaway git repositories), which runs in the same job. A mistake in a released migration is corrected by a new migration, never by editing the old one.

After pulling a change that edited an existing migration (most often `V1`), a local database that already applied the old one fails Flyway's validation (its checksum changed). Recreate it (the data is test data):

```bash
docker compose down -v && docker compose up -d   # then start the backend again
```

## Sessions

Login creates a server-side session (table `sessions`, valid for 12 hours) and sets an HttpOnly cookie with a JWT that only _names_ the session and the user. Every request looks the session up and loads the user's current role and flags from the database. So:

- logging out, deactivating or deleting a user, or changing a user's role or admin flag ends their sessions at once (the user has to log in again);
- rights claimed inside a token are ignored, and a token whose session is revoked, expired or unknown is refused with `401`, even with a valid signature;
- the application always keeps at least one active administrator: demoting, deactivating or deleting the last one answers `409`.
- a user who is the site manager of a project can be deactivated (the answer warns which projects are left without an active site manager) but not deleted (`409`) until the project has another site manager (see "Members").
- **In the SPA** every call to the API goes through `apiFetch` (`lib/api.ts`). A `401` does not throw the person to the login page, because that loses what they typed: it raises a window event, and `SessionExpiredDialog` (mounted in `AppLayout`) offers to sign in again in place. It is a modal `<dialog>` of the shared `Modal`, opened when the session ends, so it stacks **on top of** any other dialog that is open at that moment (a form with twenty meters typed into it), makes that one inert while it is there and gives the focus back when it closes; a plain overlay would be behind a dialog in the browser's top layer, out of reach, with the typed text lost on the way out. Escape does nothing: the person signs in or leaves. The page underneath is untouched, so a half-written entry stays in its form and the person repeats the action (saving, signing). A page that has unsaved work says so (`setUnsavedWork`); a page that merely failed to load (a refused `GET`, nothing typed) is reloaded after signing in. The dialog signs in the **same account only** (the name is fixed), so the page never continues under another person; if the new session needs a password change it goes to that form. Signing in again keeps the entry's draft (see "Daily reports"); the dialog's "Odhlásit" ("Odhlásit a zahodit změny" when there is unsaved work) ends the session for good and removes every draft on the device. Covered by `e2e/session-expiry.spec.ts`.

## Passwords and login limits

- **Policy** (`PasswordPolicy`): 12 to 256 characters with a lower-case letter, an upper-case letter, a digit and a special character; **not a common password** (the letters of the password, lower-cased and without diacritics, are not one of a short list of base words such as `heslo`, `qwertz`, `admin` or `stavebni`, whatever digits and symbols surround them: `Heslo123456!` and `P@ssw0rd!2024` are refused, `Heslo-Je-Dlouhe-A-Neobvykle-7!` is not); and **does not contain the account's own login name** (case-insensitive, names under 3 characters are not looked for). The change-password form shows the same checklist (`frontend/src/lib/passwordPolicy.ts`, whose word list has to be kept in step with the server's); the server enforces it. The offline list is a first line only; the breached-password check below covers the long tail but fails open.
- **Temporary passwords:** an account made by an administrator, and an account whose password an administrator reset, has `mustChangePwd`. Such a session may only call `POST /api/auth/change-password`; everything else answers `403` with `code: PASSWORD_CHANGE_REQUIRED` (the SPA then shows the change form). This is decided from the database on every request, so whoever handed out the password cannot go on acting as that user. **A generated password expires after 7 days** (`users.passwordExpiresAt`, set by account creation, administrator reset and both command-line commands, cleared when the user chooses a password): a correct but expired password is refused at login with `401` and `code: PASSWORD_EXPIRED` (only someone who knows the password sees it), no session is opened, and an administrator issues a new one. A wrong password gets the ordinary answer, expired or not.
- **Change:** needs the current password; wrong answers are limited to 5 per 15 minutes per user; all the user's other sessions end. **Reset** (administrators, `Action.UserPasswordReset`): new generated password shown once (`Cache-Control: no-store`), every session of the user ends, a login lockout of the account is lifted. An administrator may reset **10 passwords per 15 minutes** (`429` with `Retry-After`; counted per acting administrator, after the permission check, every attempt). Neither password nor hash is written to the audit log.
- **Login limits** (table `rate_limit_attempts`, failures only): 30 failures per client address and 20 per account name within 15 minutes, then `429` with `Retry-After`. An attempt is **reserved before the password is checked** (under a database lock per key) and **given back only when it succeeds**, so any number of requests that arrive at the same moment cannot all slip under the limit, a refused request is not recorded (asking again does not extend the wait), and a success removes only its own attempt, not the failures of others. A client on IPv6 is counted as its **/64 prefix** (an IPv4-mapped address as the IPv4 address): rotating the last 64 bits gains nothing. The address comes from `CLIENT_IP_HEADER` when it is configured, otherwise from the TCP peer; `X-Forwarded-For` and the request body are never trusted. A known name can therefore be locked for up to 15 minutes by someone else; an administrator reset lifts it.
- **Breached passwords:** a new password is checked against the Pwned Passwords range API (`BreachedPasswordService`) and refused when it appears in a known breach. Only the first five hex characters of its SHA-1 leave the server (k-anonymity, with padding); the password is never sent. It fails open: if the service is unreachable, slow (2 s limit) or answers nonsense, the password is accepted and a warning is logged. Passwords generated by the application are not checked.
- `users.passwordChangedAt` records when the user last _chose_ a password; a temporary password (new account, administrator or command-line reset) clears it.
- **A login cannot outlive a reset.** The password is checked outside a transaction, so a reset (which ends all sessions) could land before the session is inserted. The session is therefore opened in a transaction that locks the user row and compares the stored password hash with the one that was checked: if it changed, no session is opened and the answer is the ordinary `401`. Changing one's own password does the same: it is refused with `409` when the password was reset or changed after it was checked, instead of overwriting the newer one. A request that was already running when a reset happened still finishes (the session is looked up once per request); that window is milliseconds and the reset ends everything after it.
- Unknown, deactivated and deleted accounts take the same time as a wrong password and get the same answer. Argon2 runs off the request threads, at most 4 at a time.

## Request and upload limits

- Every request body is capped before it is read: **256 KiB** for JSON, **6 MiB** for `POST /api/photos/upload`. A body that announces more is refused with `413` before it is read; one that does not announce its size (chunked) is refused with `413` as soon as it has grown past the limit. Either way the answer is like every other one (the security headers, nothing logged as an error). The limit is `BoundedRequestBody` (`plugins/`), not Ktor's own plugin, see the bullet on how the upload is read, below.
- **Uploads in flight, in two stages** (`UploadGate`, numbers in `UploadLimits`). (1) **An upload in progress, per user: 2**, taken when the request arrives, before any of its body is read; a refused request is answered at once with `429` and `Retry-After: 5`. (2) **A body in memory, for the whole server: 8**, taken only when the file part arrives and the person has been shown to be allowed to add a photo to that entry (next bullet): somebody who may not upload never holds one, so an investor, an inspector or a stranger who sends slowly cannot fill the places of the people who work on site. At the limit: `503` with `code: UPLOAD_BUSY` and `Retry-After: 5`. The counts live in this process (they protect this process's heap). Every place is given back on every way out: the answer, an error, a body that ends early, a client that drops the connection, and two deadlines that answer `408`: **30 s** from the request to the file part (a start that never says what it is for) and **10 minutes** for the whole upload (5 MiB at about 70 kbit/s). What a body costs: up to 5 MiB read into a buffer and copied (about 10 MiB for a moment, 5 MiB after), so about 11 MiB each; 8 bodies are about 90 MiB, and the two images in process add about 50 to 80 MiB each, so roughly 250 MiB of the 400 MB heap and about 150 MiB left for the rest. That is arithmetic from the code, not a load test. The numbers are constants, not configuration.
- **The upload is read as a stream, and who may upload is decided before the file is read.** Two things in Ktor 3.6 made the obvious way wrong, both measured here. Its `RequestBodyLimit` copies the body through a channel that it never flushes, so a handler sees nothing of a request until 1 MiB has arrived (1,048,000 bytes and then silence never reach it, 1,100,000 do); `BoundedRequestBody` is the same plugin with the copy flushed, so the start of a body reaches the handler as it arrives. And `receiveMultipart` buffers every text field whole (up to the 6 MiB part limit, as bytes and then as a String) before any check. So the upload is read with Ktor's streaming parser (`parseMultipart`, in `UploadParts.kt`): text fields with a limit of a kilobyte (the rest of a longer one is dropped as it arrives, never held), and when the file part arrives, before its first byte is read, the entry is found from the fields that came before it and the membership, the `PhotoUpload` right of the role and the entry not being signed are checked. The fields `reportId` (and `projectId`) must therefore come **before** the file (the SPA sends them first; a file that comes first is a `400`). An investor, an inspector or a stranger is answered `403` with nothing of their file held. (Before, the file was read first and such a person got `400` for a bad image or `403` for a good one.)
- **Volume per person** (`RateLimiter.Rules`; `reserve` without `release`, every attempt counts, `429` with `Retry-After` and a Czech message): **60 photo uploads per 5 minutes**, counted once the upload is in progress (stage 1) and before its body is read, so a request that is refused for who is asking (a stranger, an investor) **does** count against that person's 60; a request the gate turned away for a lack of a place does not. PDF exports and administrator resets are described above and are not charged when refused for who is asking.
- An upload carries **one** image of at most **5 MiB**, at most 8 multipart parts, and text fields of at most 256 characters (`400` otherwise). The parser's limit per part is the request cap; the exact limits are checked in `UploadParts.kt`. A multipart body that is not one (no boundary, a boundary over 70 characters or with a letter outside ASCII, a part header without a colon or with an empty `Content-Disposition`, a body that ends in the middle) is a `400` with one generic Czech message that repeats nothing the client sent or the parser said (before, such requests were a `500` with a stack trace or a `409` that echoed both; the parser's job is always cancelled, whatever the outcome).
- The image type is checked by its magic bytes. Its dimensions are read from the **header** and checked **before** any pixel is decoded, so a tiny file that claims billions of pixels cannot exhaust memory: a **JPEG may claim up to 64 megapixels** (a phone photo is 12 to 50), other formats up to 16 megapixels (the JDK decodes them in full), at most 16,000 px per side; more is a `400`. A big JPEG is decoded **already reduced** (the reader skips pixels while decoding), so about 4 megapixels at most are held in memory whatever the size of the original. That holds for baseline JPEGs. A JPEG with **several scans** (progressive, and equally a sequential file with one scan per component, which the JDK's decoder treats the same way) is different in two ways. `JpegHeader` decides both from the bytes, in one pass over the whole file, before anything is decoded, and refuses a file it cannot read **the way libjpeg reads it**: it skips by their declared length only the segments libjpeg reads the same way (APPn, COM, DQT, DHT, DRI, DAC), and refuses any other marker (the reserved 02..BF, JPG0, DNL, JPG1..13). That matters: libjpeg takes a reserved marker met in entropy data at a restart boundary for garbage and scans forward to the next marker, so a "segment" of 65,535 bytes made of bare scan headers is decoded scan by scan, while a walk that skipped it by its length counted 10 scans (measured: 6,563 passes, 66 s at a megapixel; a 5 MiB file holds about 80 such segments). A single-scan file is judged by its first scan, as libjpeg reads no further:
  - **Memory:** the decoder keeps the coefficients of the whole image in native memory, about 6 bytes per pixel with 3 components: measured at **98 MiB for 16 megapixels and 374 MiB for 64**, twice with the two decode slots. So such a file may claim **16 megapixels at most** (`PhotoService.MAX_PROGRESSIVE_JPEG_PIXELS`, `400` "Progressive JPEG dimensions exceed 16MP limit"), and **3 components at most**: a fourth costs 8 bytes per pixel (the JDK decodes such files; 126 MiB against 97 MiB at 16 megapixels).
  - **Time:** every further scan makes the decoder walk every block of the image again, 0.11 s per scan at 16 megapixels, and a scan can be a dozen bytes, so 5 MiB of them would keep a native decoder, which cannot be cancelled, busy for hours. So such a file may have **32 scans at most** (`PhotoService.MAX_JPEG_SCANS`; the ordinary progressive script has 10). The worst file the airlock now accepts (16 megapixels, 4:4:4, 32 scans) decodes in **about 5 s of CPU and 104 MiB** (measured on the development machine; an ordinary 10-scan file of that size takes 2.3 s). That is the bound for one decode, and two run at a time.

  **A second layer, inside the decoder**, for the day the walk and libjpeg part ways: the decode runs with a listener that throws when it has made more than **40 passes** (`PhotoService.MAX_DECODE_PASSES`) or has run for more than **20 s** (`DECODE_DEADLINE`; the worst accepted file takes 5 s here and the machine is slower), and the answer is `400`. Measured on the crafted file above with the walk deliberately fooled: the decode stops after **0.38 s** (66 s without). An exception out of the listener stops the native decoder at once; `ImageReader.abort()` does **not** (a one-shot abort from another thread changed nothing: 56 s, because the JDK clears the flag at the start of each pass), so it is not used. `ImageDecodeLimitsTest` tests both layers, the second with a test seam that weakens the walk.

  `ProgressiveJpegMemoryTest` decodes that worst file in a JVM with a fixed 64 MiB heap and fails when two slots would need more than 256 MiB of native memory (what the 1 GB machine can spare besides the heap and Argon2). A damaged image is a `400`. (JDK limits: WebP files pass the magic-byte check but the JDK has no WebP reader, so they end as a `400`; HEIC is not supported.)

- Accepted images are decoded once and stored as new metadata-free JPEGs (fitted into 1920x1080, never scaled up) plus a 400x300 thumbnail. **The camera's orientation (EXIF) is applied before the metadata is thrown away**, so a portrait photo is not stored on its side; a damaged EXIF block never rejects a photo. The stored width and height are those of the file on disk. Image work runs off the request threads, **two images at a time**; the engine drops a connection that sends nothing for 60 seconds (`requestReadTimeoutSeconds`), and the file is read with a suspending read, which holds no thread while a slow client sends.
- A photo belongs to **the entry the request names**: `reportId` is required (no "newest entry of the project" guess), and a bare date needs `projectId` (dates repeat across projects). The form offers photo upload only for a saved, unsigned entry. Errors never repeat what the database or a library said. A stored photo is served with `Cache-Control: private, no-cache`, so a logout or a lost membership takes effect.
- **Text of users and authorised persons** is bounded too, after trimming (characters are counted as a person counts them, an emoji is one): display name 200 characters, ČKAIT number 50; authorised person: name 200, organisation 200, scope of the authorisation or function 500; the scope of a member's authorisation and responsibility 500 (`ProjectMemberService.MAX_SCOPE_CHARS`); the author's supervision of a project 500 for who it is and 200 for the number of its authorisation. One character more, or a NUL character, is `400` with a Czech message (`... může mít nejvýše 200 znaků`) and changes nothing; a blank optional field is stored as nothing. The first administrator made from the command line follows the display-name limit. The database columns are unbounded `text`, so these limits are the only bound besides the 256 KiB body.
- The database stores **relative keys** (`photos/<uuid>_orig.jpg`, `photos/<uuid>_thumb.jpg`), never absolute paths, and the API never returns a path. A key is only followed when it has exactly that shape and resolves inside `UPLOADS_DIR`; otherwise the photo answers `404`. If the database write fails, the two files already written are removed.

It is removed when the changes are saved or undone, when the person discards it, **on logout and when the session dialog's "Odhlásit" is used (all drafts), when somebody else signs in (theirs go; the same person keeps theirs)**, and when the application starts if it is older than 30 days, dated more than a day in the future, unreadable, or (when somebody is signed in) not that person's. **When nobody is signed in the start-up prune removes only the expired or damaged ones**: the login page forgets who was signed in without a logout, and the person who signs in again finds their drafts. A draft says whose it is and is never read for anybody else, and **a page keeps the draft of the person who opened it**: it compares that person with the one signed in at every write, and if another tab has signed somebody else in it writes nothing, for good (`syncDraft`). It holds personal data (the names of the people on site), so a device is a place where that data is kept, and the privacy notice (decision D13) has to say so.

A draft identical to the stored entry (the save went through and only its answer was lost) is not offered and is removed; a draft of a signed entry or of a role that is **known** not to write is never kept. A failed request for the role is not an answer: the role stays unknown, the form stays locked with "Zkusit znovu", and no draft is offered or removed until it loads. While an offer waits, saving and signing are disabled (so "Obnovit" can never put unsigned text into a signed form). "Identical" is judged after the same tidying the server does (trimmed trades and texts, names rewritten with "; ", empty trade rows and leading zeros gone: `normalizeForm`), so a save whose answer was lost does not show a false warning. The draft is compared with the entry by **the version it was typed on**, not by the clock (a phone's clock can be minutes off the server's): when the entry has a different version now (somebody saved since), the banner says so, because restoring and saving would replace their text. The save itself (`expectedUpdatedAt`, `expectNew`, `STALE_VERSION`) is unchanged.
      - **Without storage the page still works** (every access is guarded; a full disk or a private window just means no draft, and the leave question says the text will be lost). Photos are not part of a draft (they are uploaded at once, for a saved entry). Covered by `e2e/report-draft.spec.ts`, `e2e/session-expiry.spec.ts` and the unit tests of `draftStore` and `reportForm`.

### Roles: what a person may do follows their role in the project

A person has **two kinds of role**, and they do different things:

- the **role in a project** (`project_members.role`: BOSS = stavbyvedoucí / project manager, WORKER, INSPECTOR, INVESTOR) decides **everything they may do inside that project**: the same person can be the manager of one project and an ordinary worker in another. It is passed to every permission decision (`Resource.role`) and read from the database inside the transaction, so a changed role applies to the very next request. The API shows it as `myRole` on a project (null for an administrator who is not a member), and the SPA offers actions by it;
- the **global role** of the account (`users.role`) is the role a person usually has: it is the default offered when adding them to a project, and it decides **who may create a project** (global BOSS, because the creator becomes the project's manager). It gives no right inside a project.
- the **administrator flag** is independent of both: it opens user administration and the audit log, lets the person read every project, and is the audited way in (below). It gives no write right in a project.

What each project role may do (the matrix is pinned by `RbacTest`):

|                                                               | BOSS                                   | WORKER | INSPECTOR               | INVESTOR                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| write a report / add a photo                                  | yes                                    | yes    | no                      | no                      |
| correct a report                                              | any                                    | own    | no                      | no                      |
| sign a report (needs a ČKAIT number)                          | yes                                    | no     | no                      | no                      |
| delete a photo (permitted, but no route exists yet)           | yes                                    | no     | no                      | no                      |
| acknowledge a signed report                                   | no                                     | no     | yes                     | yes                     |
| sign a site handover protocol (one side each, see below)      | yes (the contractor's side; ČKAIT)     | no     | yes (the client's side) | yes (the client's side) |
| manage members and authorized persons                         | yes (and an administrator)             | no     | no                      | no                      |
| change the identification of the diary (`PUT /projects/{id}`) | yes (only the manager of THIS project) | no     | no                      | no                      |
| replace the site manager (`PUT /projects/{id}/site-manager`)  | yes (only the manager of THIS project) | no     | no                      | no                      |

**Members:**

- Only the project's manager, or an application administrator as the audited way in (decision D1), may add a member or change a role. Nobody changes their own role (an administrator who joined as a worker cannot promote themselves afterwards).
- **The scope of authorisation and responsibility** (backlog C2, příloha 12 A d)) is a text of at most 500 characters on the membership of a manager (BOSS) or of the supervision (INSPECTOR): what the person ensures on this build and answers for. The project's manager sets it when adding or changing a member (`POST /projects/{id}/members`, `scope`); a request that does not mention it keeps what the member has, an empty text clears it, a scope for any other role is `400`, and a change to another role drops it. A manager may state their **own** scope (a project with one manager has nobody else to do it); changing their own role is still refused. The change is audited with the scope before and after (`project.member.add`, the `scope` is left out of the row when there is none). The person's name and ČKAIT number are not copied to the membership: they come from the account at the moment a part A snapshot is made. A project has at most **100** members who are BOSS or INSPECTOR (as the list of members shows them: a membership of a deleted user is not counted) and at most **100** active authorised persons (`409` when another is added), so that the snapshot stored with every signature stays small.
- The project's **site manager** (`projects.siteManagerId`) can be neither removed nor given another role, and a project always keeps a BOSS membership: the check counts **active** users only, a manager whose account is deactivated or deleted does not count as another manager. The role of a membership has **no database default** (migration V7): a forgotten column must not make someone a manager who may sign the diary.
- **Creating a project (decision D2):** `createProject` names a site manager, who must exist, be active (not deactivated, not deleted) and hold a ČKAIT number, otherwise `409` with a message that says why (the same `409` as when the site manager is replaced; the creator, who becomes a manager too, needs a number as well). The creator **and the site manager are added as BOSS members** of the project, whatever the role of their account, so the site manager is protected and able to sign from the first moment (the `project.create` audit row names the site manager, and each BOSS membership given at creation is a `project.member.add` row of its own, in the shape of adding a member: the project, the user, no role before and BOSS after). The SPA offers only people with a number as site manager (`hasCkait` in the user options) and says so for the others.
- **A site manager who is a user** can always be **deactivated**: it is the emergency switch for a compromised or departed site manager, and their sessions end. The answer (`POST /users/{id}/deactivate`) then carries a `warning` that names the projects left without an active site manager and says to have the project's manager replace the site manager (`PUT /projects/{id}/site-manager`). Until that is done an entry of the project can be signed only by another active manager (BOSS) of it who holds a ČKAIT number: signing does not need the site manager in particular, but it does need an active, qualified manager. **Deleting** such a user is refused with `409` that names the project(s); after the replacement they can be deleted like anybody else.
- **Replacing the site manager** (`PUT /projects/{id}/site-manager`, body `{siteManagerId, reason?, updatedAt}`):
  - Only the manager of the project may do this; the new one must be an active member whose role in the project is BOSS and who holds a ČKAIT number (decision D2), otherwise `409`.
  - The version (`updatedAt`) is required like for any change of the project (`400` without it, `409 STALE_VERSION` when the project changed meanwhile).
  - The previous site manager stays a manager of the project, and from then on only the NEW site manager is protected against removal and demotion.
  - The audit row `project.site_manager.change` keeps who it was and who it is, with their ČKAIT numbers, and the reason. The entry PDF names the site manager in the identification.
- **Project membership and administrators:**
  - Project-scoped endpoints require project membership. App admins can **read** every project, but being an admin gives no right to write: creating, overwriting, signing or acknowledging a report, uploading or removing a photo, and creating, changing, deleting or signing a site handover protocol all require real membership of that project. The membership is judged inside the transaction, on rows locked `FOR UPDATE`, never assumed.
  - **Joining a project as an administrator is the audited way in** (decision D1): a project manager of the project, or an administrator, may add members (an administrator adds themselves), and the audit row names the project, the user and the role before and after.
  - An administrator cannot change their own role (they could otherwise make themselves a project manager and then a member of any project); changing someone's role or administrator flag ends their sessions and is audited with before and after, and fixing a display name does not log anyone out.

**Signed records are immutable in the database too** (migration V5, triggers that compare the whole row): a signed daily report cannot be changed or deleted (V11 later made that every column, the signer and the part A snapshot of format 3 included; the acknowledgements are a table of their own), photos cannot be added to, changed on or removed from a signed report, and a handover protocol that either party has signed cannot be changed or deleted (the protocol's trigger looks for a row in `site_handover_signatures`, which is append-only itself: see "Site handovers"). The application checks all of this first; a photo whose processing was still running when the report was signed is refused at insert time, not given to the signed record. The audit rows of photos, handovers, authorised persons and members carry the entity id and before/after snapshots (the `photo.upload` row holds the SHA-256 of both stored files; `PhotoService.deletePhoto`, which no route calls yet, would record them as null).

### Daily reports: signing and acknowledging

- **Signing (`POST /projects/{id}/reports/{idOrDate}/sign` and `POST /reports/{reportId}/sign`):**
  - Body: `{"password": "…", "expectedUpdatedAt": "…"}`.
  - **Qualification (decision D2):** Only a member whose role **in that project** is BOSS may sign (a global BOSS who is only a worker here may not), **and who holds a ČKAIT number**: without one the answer is `403` with `code: SIGNER_NOT_QUALIFIED` and a message that says why. The same number is required to be made a manager of a project (adding a member as BOSS, naming a site manager, creating a project) so that nobody is given a role they cannot use.
  - **Editing a ČKAIT number (`PATCH /users/{id}`, administrators):** allowed, to set, correct or clear it (an authorisation can be revoked or suspended, a number can be mistyped), and every change is audited with the number as it was and as it became (`user.update`, `UserSnapshot`). When the number really changed and the person is the site manager or a BOSS member of any project that is not deleted, the answer carries a `warning` (shown by the SPA after saving; not stored, not in the audit row) that names the projects. **An administrator cannot change, give themselves or clear their own number** (`409`; sending it back unchanged is no change), so that qualifying oneself takes a second, visible, audited act (another account) instead of one edit: a speed bump, not a barrier (see Follow-ups). Setting the number of the only administrator of a fresh installation is the operator command `set-ckait`. Signing reads the number at the moment of signing and stores it with the signature (format 3, see "What a signature covers"), so clearing or correcting it later changes no entry that is already signed.
  - **Password re-entry (decision D8):** Signing asks for the password again so a session that was left open or stolen cannot sign. The routes first decide whether the person may sign this entry at all (a worker is told `403` without being asked for a password), then check the password (`400` "Heslo není správné", never `401`, which would look like a lapsed session; at most 5 wrong answers per 15 minutes per person, then `429`; a right password gives its attempt back, like a login), then sign in one transaction.
  - **Signed once & numbered at signing (decision D11):** A report is signed **exactly once**: a second request answers `409` and changes nothing (signer and time stay). The number of the entry in the diary is given at that moment: the Nth signed entry of a project is number N, so the signed entries follow one another without gaps however many drafts were started and abandoned; a draft has no number (`sequenceNumber` is `null` in the API, empty in the CSV of `CsvExportService`, which has no route, `"zatím bez čísla"` on a draft PDF). The number is assigned under the audit lock, is part of what the signature covers, and is checked by a database trigger (V13: the number given at signing must be the next one of the project; a signed entry must have one; a draft cannot be given one). Entries signed before V13 keep the number they were signed with. Numbers follow the order of signing, not the order of the days. A non-member gets `403`.
- **What a signature covers:**
  - In the statement that locks the entry the server stores `daily_reports.signatureHash`: the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON (sorted keys, compact) of the entry, built from stored rows only. **Format 3**, in which new signatures are made, covers:
    - the entry: id, project, date, number, author, work description, the workers list with the **names**, control-day flag, construction object, late-entry flag and reason, the **weather as its text**, and the **nine fields of the vyhláška** (`FORMAT_3_DETAIL_KEYS`: the eight of format 2 and `workStateChanges`, "Zahájení, přerušení a obnovení prací (data a důvody)", příloha 12 B.2 b));
    - **who signed and when**: `signedById`, and **the signer's name and ČKAIT number as they were in the transaction that signed** (`signerName`, `signerCkaitNumber`, stored in the columns of the same names);
    - **the identification of the diary as it stood at that moment** (`partASha256`, the SHA-256 of `daily_reports.partASnapshot`, see below);
    - the **SHA-256 of every photo** (recorded at upload in `photos.sha256` and `photos.thumbSha256`).
  - **The weather is covered as its text** (`WeatherData.describe()`: `zataženo, 8,5 až 15 °C`, or `null` when nothing is stated), the text that the PDF and the audit snapshot print, not the stored decimals (formats 1 and 2 hash the stored JSON). A number's textual form can therefore never change what is hashed, whatever `jsonb` or a library does to a decimal. The function is frozen with the format: `GoldenSignatureTest` pins its output, and another wording needs a format 4.
  - **The signer is frozen** (backlog C2, finding N1): `signerName` is the display name (trimmed) and `signerCkaitNumber` the ČKAIT number that the signer's account had when it was signed. Both are set in the statement that locks the entry, under the audit lock, and frozen by the signed-records triggers with every other column. Renaming the user, or correcting or clearing their number, changes nothing about an entry that is already signed: the signature check and the PDF print the stored values. Formats 1 and 2 (test data only) cannot be repaired and still read both from the account as it is now. **Not frozen by format 3, and left for backlog B9:** the author of the entry, the uploader of a photo, and the people named under an acknowledgement, an addendum or an entry of another party, whose names (and numbers) the PDF reads from their accounts as they are now.
  - **Part A as it stood on the day of signing** (`daily_reports.partASnapshot`, `TEXT`, never `jsonb`, hashed exactly as stored, M10): a canonical JSON text (sorted keys, compact), built from the database in the signing transaction (`PartASnapshot.build`) and stored in the same statement that signs. It holds:
    - `identification`: the project's name, address, cadastral area, parcel numbers, builder, contractor, permit number and date, designer, **author's supervision** (`authorSupervisionName`, `authorSupervisionAuthorization`; příloha 12 A e)), TDS, BOZP coordinator, contract number and date, design documentation version and date, subcontractors and supporting documents (dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`);
    - `siteManager`: `userId`, `name`, `ckaitNumber`, as the account had them;
    - `members`: every member whose role in the project is **BOSS or INSPECTOR** (users not deleted), oldest membership first, each with `userId`, `name`, `role`, `ckaitNumber` and `scope` (the scope of the authorisation and responsibility, A d));
    - `authorizedPersons`: **only the active ones** (not revoked at that moment), oldest first, with `id`, `name`, `company` and `authorization` (the function, A f));
    - `projectId`.

    Its size is bounded: every text has the limit of the field it comes from, a project has at most 100 members who are BOSS or INSPECTOR (counted as the list of members shows them: the membership of a user who was deleted is neither listed nor counted, since nobody could remove it) and at most 100 active authorised persons (adding another is `409`), and a snapshot of more than 1 MiB is refused at signing with `409` and a message, rolled back, with nothing signed and nothing recorded. **Ordinary text always fits:** the largest part A that the limits allow, with 100 + 100 persons and every text at its limit made of four-byte characters, is about 739 KB. **What can exceed 1 MiB is control characters,** which the canonical text writes as six bytes each (`\u0001`): a full project (100 + 100 persons) with every field at its limit and made of nothing else comes to about 1.05 MiB. Two kinds of people can cause it together: a project's manager (the scope of each member, the authorised persons, the identification) and an administrator (the display names and ČKAIT numbers of the accounts); it is not a case that comes up by accident, and the answer to it is the refusal, not a larger limit. The workers, the weather and the other fields of the entry are not part of it.

  - **Signature format (`daily_reports.signatureFormat`, V14):** `1` for what was signed before the vyhláška detail fields existed, `2` for an entry signed with them (until format 3), and `3` for an entry signed with the signer and part A frozen; the server sets it at the moment of signing, before the hash is made, and checks an entry in the format it was signed in. Each format is frozen: `GoldenSignatureTest` pins the exact content and hash of formats 1 and 2, `SignatureFormat3VectorsTest` and `scripts/signature/verify-signature-format-3.py` those of format 3 (computed independently of the code); `FORMAT_2_DETAIL_KEYS` and `FORMAT_3_DETAIL_KEYS` are lists of keys that never change, and a test fails when `EntryDetails`, or a column of the project, of a membership, of an authorised person or of an entry, gets a field that no format covers. A field added later needs a **format 4**, never a change to an existing format, and **a key is never renamed**: it is part of what was signed. From format 2 on a missing hash is a failure, not "nothing to compare"; in format 3 a missing signer name, number or part A is a failure as well (the database refuses to store such an entry too: a check constraint of V14; and the signed-records triggers, V5 and V11, freeze every column afterwards).
  - **The exact bytes of format 3**, for a verifier that is not this code (the whole-diary export, backlog C5, ships one). It needs only the values stored on the entry and its photos, and `partASnapshot` **as the text it is stored in**: the content is built from them as below, written as canonical JSON (sorted keys, no white space, `"` and `\` and control characters escaped, every other character as it is, UTF-8) and hashed. `signatureHash` is the SHA-256 of that text, `partASha256` the SHA-256 of the stored `partASnapshot`; the signing time is `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ` in UTC. **White space, and so "blank" and "trimmed", means exactly Kotlin's `Char.isWhitespace()`** (what the code uses to decide that a detail field says nothing and to trim the weather condition): the 28 characters U+0009 to U+000D, U+001C to U+001F, U+0020, U+00A0, U+1680, U+2000 to U+200A, U+2028, U+2029, U+202F, U+205F and U+3000, and no others. In particular **U+0085 (NEL) is not white space** here, although Python's `str.strip()` and `str.isspace()` say it is, and neither are U+180E, U+200B and U+FEFF; a verifier in another language must use that list, not its own idea of white space (the vectors pin U+0085, NBSP, the ideographic space, a backslash, a quotation mark, an astral character and control characters, and fail with Python's own `strip()`). The other choice, refusing C1 control characters when a text is entered, was not taken: it would change what may be written in a legal record, and the specification can say what the code does. The reference verifier is `scripts/signature/verify-signature-format-3.py` (`python3 verify-signature-format-3.py vectors <file>` recomputes the golden vectors in `backend/src/test/resources/signature-format-3-vectors.json`, which `SignatureFormat3VectorsTest` reads too; `entry <file>` prints the hash of an entry); a test runs it, and another checks that the block below is the one in the script, so this text cannot drift from the code:

    ```python
    import hashlib
    import json
    from decimal import Decimal

    # The fields of the vyhláška that format 3 covers, by key. Never changes: a tenth field is a format 4.
    DETAIL_KEYS = [
        "materials", "machinery", "testsAndChecks", "safetyNotes", "dustMeasures",
        "accessibilityMeasures", "defects", "workStateChanges", "otherNotes",
    ]


    # What "white space" and "blank" mean in this specification is the application's (Kotlin's Char.isWhitespace(), which is Java's
    # Character.isWhitespace(c) || Character.isSpaceChar(c)), and NOT Python's str.strip() or str.isspace(): these 28 characters and no
    # others. Python also counts U+0085 (NEL) as white space; here it is an ordinary character, like U+180E, U+200B and U+FEFF.
    WHITESPACE = (
        "\u0009\u000a\u000b\u000c\u000d\u001c\u001d\u001e\u001f\u0020\u00a0\u1680"
        "\u2000\u2001\u2002\u2003\u2004\u2005\u2006\u2007\u2008\u2009\u200a\u2028\u2029\u202f\u205f\u3000"
    )


    def trim(text):
        """The text without the white space at both ends."""
        return text.strip(WHITESPACE)


    def is_blank(text):
        """A text that is empty or only white space."""
        return trim(text) == ""


    def canonical(value):
        """Sorted keys, no white space, the characters a JSON string must escape (quotation mark, backslash, U+0000 to U+001F) escaped
        as json.dumps does, every other character as it is."""
        return json.dumps(value, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"), ensure_ascii=False)


    def sha256_hex(text):
        """SHA-256 of the UTF-8 bytes of a text, as 64 lower-case hex characters."""
        return hashlib.sha256(text.encode("utf-8")).hexdigest()


    def degrees(number):
        """A temperature as people write it: no trailing zeros, a decimal comma ("8,5", "15", "-3", "0")."""
        value = Decimal(repr(float(number)))
        if value == 0:
            return "0"
        return format(value.normalize(), "f").replace(".", ",")


    def weather_text(weather):
        """The weather as the text that is signed, or None when nothing is stated (the text of WeatherData.describe())."""
        if not weather:
            return None
        parts = []
        condition = trim(weather.get("condition") or "")
        if condition:
            parts.append(condition)
        low, high = weather.get("tempMin"), weather.get("tempMax")
        if low is not None and high is not None:
            parts.append("%s až %s °C" % (degrees(low), degrees(high)))
        elif low is not None:
            parts.append("min. %s °C" % degrees(low))
        elif high is not None:
            parts.append("max. %s °C" % degrees(high))
        return ", ".join(parts) or None


    def blank_to_none(text):
        """A field that is empty or only white space says nothing: null. Any other text is signed exactly as it is stored."""
        return text if text is not None and not is_blank(text) else None


    def content(entry, part_a_text):
        """The content that is hashed: `entry` holds the values stored on the entry and its photos, `part_a_text` is the text
        stored in `partASnapshot`, exactly as it is stored (it is never built again)."""
        photos = [
            {"id": p["id"], "sha256": p["sha256"], "thumbSha256": p["thumbSha256"], "uploadedById": p["uploadedById"]}
            for p in entry["photos"]
            if not p.get("removed")
        ]
        details = entry.get("details") or {}
        return {
            "format": 3,
            "id": entry["id"],
            "projectId": entry["projectId"],
            "date": entry["date"],
            "sequenceNumber": entry["sequenceNumber"],
            "authorId": entry["authorId"],
            "workDescription": entry["workDescription"] or "",
            "workersByTrade": entry["workersByTrade"],
            "isControlDay": entry["isControlDay"],
            "constructionObj": entry["constructionObj"],
            "weather": weather_text(entry["weather"]),
            "isLateEntry": entry["isLateEntry"],
            "lateEntryReason": entry["lateEntryReason"],
            "signedAt": entry["signedAt"],
            "signedById": entry["signedById"],
            "signerName": entry["signerName"],
            "signerCkaitNumber": entry["signerCkaitNumber"],
            "partASha256": sha256_hex(part_a_text),
            "details": {key: blank_to_none(details.get(key)) for key in DETAIL_KEYS},
            "photos": sorted(photos, key=lambda p: p["id"]),
        }


    def signature_hash(entry, part_a_text):
        """The signature: the SHA-256 of the canonical text of the content."""
        return sha256_hex(canonical(content(entry, part_a_text)))
    ```

  - **What the database enforces, and what it cannot** (stated plainly): the check constraint of V14 and the triggers of V5 and V11 enforce the **shape** of a format-3 row: that it is locked and that the signer's name, the number and the snapshot are present and not blank, and that nothing of the row changes afterwards. They do **not** enforce that `signerName` and `signerCkaitNumber` equal the signer's user row, that `signedById` is a manager of the project, that the snapshot is the project's part A or that `signatureHash` is the hash of the content: only the application does that, in the transaction that signs. A **compromised application role** (whoever holds its database credentials, or the application's code) can therefore write a consistent forged signature, the signed row and its `report.lock` row together, and nothing in the database refuses it. What protects against that is the chain and its anchor, and only for what was recorded **before** the compromise: a row that was anchored (`audit-head`, the nightly record with its RFC 3161 token) cannot be replaced by a consistent forgery without the anchor finding the chain changed; a forgery made after the last anchor is not found by anything in the database. That is the limit of this design, not a bug to be closed with one more trigger (a trigger would be switched off by the same owner who is trusted with the schema, see "Database roles").
  - **Part A is frozen at the click, not at what the signer saw** (a known residual, backlog C2b): `expectedUpdatedAt` guards the entry, and the sign dialog does not show part A, so a co-manager who changes a scope, a person or the contractor between the moment the signer looked at the project and the moment they press "Podepsat" has changed what is signed, and nothing tells the signer. The snapshot is built from the database inside the signing transaction (that is the consistent moment); a snapshot of what the signer reviewed needs the dialog to show part A and the request to carry its digest (a different part A is `409 STALE_VERSION`). Until then the PDF printed after signing shows what was frozen, and a signer who wants to be sure checks the project page just before signing.
  - **Verification (`GET /reports/{id}/signature`, members and administrators):** Hashes the entry again and answers who signed (for format 3 with the **stored** name and ČKAIT number, `signerFrozen: true`; for formats 1 and 2 with the account's current ones), whether `contentMatches`, and whether the photo files on disk still hash to what was recorded (`photoFilesMatch`): a change made to a row or a file **behind the application's back**, which the audit log alone would only show if the whole chain were rewritten as well, is found. The entry page offers "Ověřit podpis"; the PDF has a signature block with the signer, the time (Prague time), the hash and the hash of the audit row that recorded the signature (the `report.lock` row, whose `after` snapshot also holds the format, the signer's name and number and the SHA-256 of the part A snapshot). Entries signed before the hash existed (test data only) have none and say so.
  - This is a **content hash bound to a password re-entry, not a qualified electronic signature**: that is a separate decision (needs the lawyer's sign-off, see the Release gate).
- **Addenda (`GET/POST /reports/{id}/addenda`):**
  - How a **signed** entry is corrected or completed: a signed entry cannot be changed, so a mistake or a missing remark stands next to it as a new record with its author and time.
  - Members whose role in the project is BOSS or WORKER may add one (an inspector, an investor, an administrator who is not a member and a stranger may not; they may read); the text is 1 to 4000 characters; an entry that is still being written takes none (it is edited directly, `409`).
  - The table is **append-only in the database** (migration V9: no update, no delete, and an insert only for a signed entry), each addendum is audited (`report.addendum.add`, the whole text in the audit row) and appears in the PDF under "Dodatky". An addendum is not part of what the signature covers (it comes after it).
- **Entries of other parties (`GET/POST /reports/{id}/remarks`; technical supervision, author's supervision, client, authorities):**
  - Made **also after the day's entry was signed** and never changed or deleted (append-only in the database, migration V10, which also adds `remarks.externalAuthor` and the type `EXTERNAL_ENTRY`).
  - A member whose role in the project is INSPECTOR or INVESTOR writes in their own name; an authority or a person without an account does not write at all: **the project's manager (BOSS) records the entry for them and has to name them** (decision D9; the entry shows "Name of the party (zapsal manager)", and is marked official).
  - A manager cannot add an entry without naming a party (the manager's own words belong in the daily entry); an inspector or investor cannot write for somebody else; a worker, an administrator who is not a member and a stranger cannot write (403); members and administrators may read. 1 to 4000 characters, the party's name up to 200.
  - Each entry is audited (`report.remark.add`, the whole text and who spoke for whom) and appears in the PDF under "Zápisy dalších osob". It is not part of what the signature covers, and it does not change it.
  - **Not done yet:** attaching the scanned or digitally signed protocol of an authority (a non-image file upload airlock).
- **Acknowledgements (`POST …/acknowledge`):**
  - Records that **a party** has taken note of the entry: **every** member whose role in the project is INSPECTOR or INVESTOR (the technical supervision, the author's supervision, the client) does so on their own behalf and **once** (a second try by the same person is `409`, the first record stays), and each is on record in the table `report_acknowledgements` (migration V11: append-only, insert only for a signed entry, one row per person and entry; it replaces the single acknowledgement of the old scheme, whose two columns were dropped and moved over).
  - It only works on a **signed** report (otherwise `409`). The entry lists all acknowledgements (`acknowledgements`, oldest first, with name, role and time; `isAcknowledged` is true when there is any), the PDF has a "Seznámení se záznamem" section, and each one is audited (`report.acknowledge`). `DashboardService` would count signed entries that nobody has taken note of, but it has no route. Since V11 **a signed daily report cannot change in any column at all** (the old exception for the acknowledgement is gone).
- `GET/POST /reports/{reportId}/…` take a report id only; a bare date is `400` (a date is only unique within a project, so use the project-scoped route). A report id of another project is `404` through `/projects/{id}/reports/{reportId}`.
- Every report change is written to the audit log in the same transaction, with the real report id and a before/after snapshot (`report.create`, `report.update`, `report.lock`, `report.acknowledge`).

### Site handovers: two parties, two signatures (backlog A3)

A handover protocol (předání a převzetí staveniště, příloha 12 B.2 a); `GET/POST /projects/{id}/handovers`, `GET/PUT/DELETE /handovers/{id}`, `POST /handovers/{id}/sign`) is an act of **two parties**: the contractor takes the site over, the client hands it over (or the other way round when the work is handed back). So it carries **one signature for each side** and is complete only when both exist.

- **Who signs which side** (decided inside the transaction, on locked rows, from the role in THIS project, never from the account's global role):
  - **Contractor (`CONTRACTOR`):** a member whose role in the project is BOSS **and who holds a ČKAIT number** (decision D2; the number is stored with the signature). Without one the answer is `403` with `code: SIGNER_NOT_QUALIFIED` and a message that says why. The reason: the takeover of the site is the act of the person who then answers for the execution of the build (the stavbyvedoucí, § 164 and § 155 of the act), the same person who signs the entries, and every manager has to hold a number anyway to be added. That is this project's reading and goes to the lawyer with the other questions; it is one check and can be dropped without touching anything else.
  - **Client (`CLIENT`):** a member whose role is INVESTOR or INSPECTOR (the technical supervision acts for the builder). No number is required and none is stored.
  - A WORKER, a person who is not a member and an administrator who is not a member get `403`. The side follows from the role: the body of the request may name `side`, and a side that is not the person's is `403`.
  - One signature per side (a second attempt is `409`), and one person never signs both sides (`409`; possible only if somebody's role in the project was changed between the two signatures, and the database refuses it too: a unique key). Any manager of the project may sign the contractor's side, whoever made the protocol; the first to sign is the contractor's signature.
- **Password re-entry (decision D8),** exactly like signing an entry: the route first decides whether the person may sign this protocol at all, **before it looks at the body** (a worker is told `403` without being asked for a password, and also when the body has no version or names a side that does not exist; for somebody who may sign, a missing or unreadable version, or a side that does not exist, is `400`, and a side that is not theirs `403`; a body that is not JSON is `400` for everybody, as for an entry), then checks the password (`400` "Heslo není správné", never `401`; 5 wrong answers per 15 minutes per person, **one counter shared with the signing of entries**, then `429`; a right password gives its attempt back), then signs in one transaction with the audit row.
- **A signature covers a version.** A protocol carries `updatedAt`. Changing it (`PUT`) and signing it (`POST …/sign`, body `{side?, password, expectedUpdatedAt}`) both **require** `expectedUpdatedAt`: `400` without it, `409` with `code: STALE_VERSION` when the stored version is another (compared on the locked row; nothing is changed). Signing does not change `updatedAt`: it is the version of the _content_, and a signature does not change the content, so the second party is not told "stale" because the first one signed.
- **The content is frozen from the first signature**: no change and no delete from the moment either party has signed (`409`; the database refuses it as well, V5: the protocol's trigger looks for a signature, and the signatures are an append-only table). An unsigned protocol may be changed by the manager (or by the worker who made it) and deleted (the row stays, with `deletedAt`). A mistake in a protocol that one party already signed cannot be corrected: a new protocol is made (there are no addenda to a protocol and no withdrawal of a signature), and a protocol that only one party signed stays in the list as one that is not complete.
  - **What the database holds** (`site_handover_signatures`, defined in `V1`, guarded in `V5`; no new migration, see "jOOQ code generation"): one row for each signature with the side, the signer, their name and role in the project and (the contractor) their ČKAIT number as they were at signing, the time and the two hashes. Check constraints refuse a row of the wrong shape (the contractor is a BOSS with a number, the client an INVESTOR or INSPECTOR with none stored, hashes of 64 hex characters, a name that is not blank); unique keys give one signature for each side and one side for each person; the trigger of the signatures refuses any change or delete and a signature on a deleted protocol; the foreign keys are `RESTRICT`, so neither the protocol nor the person can be removed from under a signature. **What it cannot know:** that the person really held that role in the project, or that a hash is the hash of the content: only the application does that, in the transaction that signs (and `verify-signatures` finds a hash that does not fit).
- **What a signature covers** (format 1, frozen: a new field needs a format 2). The content is built from the stored row, never from the request, and written as canonical JSON (sorted keys, compact; `AuditHash.canonicalJson`, the writer of the audit chain): `{"format":1,"id","projectId","type","date","participants","meterStates":[{"medium","serialNumber","state"}…],"notes"}` (`date` as `YYYY-MM-DD`, no meters as `[]`, no notes as `null`); its SHA-256 is the **`contentHash`**. Each signature then has a **`signatureHash`**, the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of `{"format":1,"side","contentSha256","signedAt","signedById","signerName","signerRole","signerCkaitNumber"}` (`signedAt` in UTC with milliseconds; the number only for the contractor, otherwise `null`). Both are stored with the signature; the `contentHash` of the two parties is the same, and the second signature first checks that the first one's hashes still fit the content as it is now (a content changed behind the application's back is refused with `409`, and `verify-signatures` reports it). Like the entry's, this is a content hash tied to a password re-entry, not a qualified electronic signature (see the Release gate).
- **Text limits** (after trimming, characters counted as a person counts them, a NUL character refused; `400` with a Czech message and nothing changed): the type 200 (required), the participants 2 000, the notes 5 000, at most 50 meters, and for each meter the medium, the serial number and the state 100. The day is a calendar day, read as the day of an entry is: a time part that is sent with it is dropped, never converted (`2026-09-11T00:30:00+02:00` is the 11th). A blank note is stored as nothing, and so is a row of meters that says nothing (no meters at all is stored as none, and signed as `[]`).
- **The screen** (`Handovers.tsx`): the form and the sign dialog are modal dialogs of the shared `Modal` (the focus goes back to the button that opened one, Escape closes it and the password field is empty when it is opened again; after a signature, whose button is gone, the focus is on the protocol that changed). **A form that holds typed text is unsaved work** (`setUnsavedWork`), so a lapsed session never costs it: the session dialog opens above the form and the form is still there, with its text, when the person has signed in again. A change that is refused because the protocol changed meanwhile (`STALE_VERSION`) or was signed meanwhile (`409`) **keeps the form open with the typed text** and offers "Načíst aktuální verzi": only when the person chooses it does the form take the stored protocol, and what they had typed stays in view under it ("Vaše úpravy, které se neuložily", with a button that puts it back for a deliberate overwrite, unless the protocol is signed by then). A signature that is refused for the same reason closes its dialog (it holds only a password) and says so, with a button that loads the list again.
- **The state** the API reports (`state`) is `DRAFT`, `SIGNED_BY_CONTRACTOR`, `SIGNED_BY_CLIENT` or `COMPLETE`, derived from the signatures (nothing stores it); `contractorSignature` and `clientSignature` say who signed, in which role, with which ČKAIT number (the contractor), when, and the two hashes.
- **Audit:** `siteHandover.create`, `.update`, `.delete` and `.sign` (entity id = the protocol; the project is named by the snapshot, `AuditProject`). The snapshots hold the content and both signatures (never a password); one signature is one `siteHandover.sign` row whose after-snapshot shows the signature that was added. The signer's name and ČKAIT number are in the permanent chain, like those of an entry's signer (see "Audit log").
- **A known residual: the identification of the diary is not part of the signature.** The content hash binds the project by its id only. A signature of an entry (format 3) freezes part A, the name, builder, contractor and persons of the project as they were that day; a protocol does not, so renaming the project or changing its builder or contractor afterwards changes what a signed protocol appears to say (the page, and one day the PDF, show the project as it is now) while its hash still verifies. It is not built, because whether it is needed is the lawyer's question 4 (`docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md`, "Part A"); if it is, it is a format 2 that covers a snapshot of the identification, made as `PartASnapshot` is.
- **Not done:** printing the protocols in the entry PDF (backlog B9); the client's person has to be an INVESTOR or INSPECTOR _member_ (a representative of the builder without an account cannot sign); photos on a protocol; an export.

## Entry dates and late entries

A new daily entry is **on time** for today and for any day since the previous working day (Monday to Friday; public holidays are not considered): on a Monday that is Friday, Saturday, Sunday and Monday. An entry for an **earlier** day is a **late entry**: it needs a reason (at most 1,000 characters), is flagged (`isLateEntry`, `lateEntryReason`) and the flag and reason are part of the record, the audit snapshot and the PDF (`Pozdní zápis:`). An entry for a day in the **future** is refused. "Today" is the date in Prague (decision D10). Only a _new_ entry is checked: correcting an existing one is not a new entry, and the reason of a late entry stays. A flagged entry without a reason cannot exist in the database either (check constraint, migration V3). The form asks for the reason when it opens a day before the previous working day, and does not offer a date in the future.

**This is not the rule of příloha 12 (part C, point 2)**, which asks for an entry on the day, at the latest on the next day on which work is done on the site (up to one working week for technically simple buildings, with the building authority's consent). The two differ: work on a Saturday makes an entry for Friday that is written on Sunday or Monday late under the decree, but not here; after a stoppage the decree allows the last day to be entered when work resumes, while the app flags it; public holidays are ignored; and only the creation of an entry is checked, so a draft created on time and signed days later is never flagged. Which rule to apply, and whether to check at signing, is a question for the lawyer (Release gate).

## Weather

The weather of a day is **entered by hand** (decision D12): a free-text condition (the form suggests common ones), the lowest and the highest temperature. Everything is optional; an entry with no value is stored as no weather, and sending an empty `weather` object on a save clears it, while leaving `weather` out keeps what is stored. Temperatures must lie between -60 and 60 °C and the lowest cannot exceed the highest; the text is at most 200 characters. The PDF shows it as readable text (`zataženo, 8,5 až 15 °C`, or `Neuvedeno`), and the audit snapshot keeps that same text, because snapshots hold no decimals (a number's textual form could change on the way through `jsonb` and break the hash chain). **The signature of format 3 covers that text too**, not the stored decimals (see "What a signature covers"), and the wording of `WeatherData.describe()` is frozen with it. **Not done:** filling the weather in automatically from Open-Meteo; its free API is non-commercial, so that needs a commercial key first. `WeatherService` still exists for that purpose but nothing calls it.

The text fields of an entry are one list in the code (`EntryDetails`): the keys of the API, the limit (5 000 characters each), what is stored, what the audit snapshot covers and what the PDF prints all follow from it. **What the signature covers does not follow it**: each signature format covers a frozen list of fields (`ReportSignature.FORMAT_2_DETAIL_KEYS`, `FORMAT_3_DETAIL_KEYS`), so adding a field to `EntryDetails` needs a new format that covers it (a test fails until that is done), otherwise the new field would not be signed. **A signature says which fields it covers** (`signatureFormat`, migration V14): format 1 is an entry as it was before these fields existed, format 2 covers eight of them, format 3 all nine (`workStateChanges` is the ninth), so an entry signed in format 1 or 2 still verifies. ## Cross-site requests

The session cookie is `SameSite=Lax`. In addition, `CrossSiteRequestGuard` refuses every state-changing request (anything but GET, HEAD, OPTIONS) under `/api/` that a browser makes on behalf of another site, with `403`:

1. an `Origin` listed in `CORS_ALLOWED_ORIGINS` is accepted;
2. otherwise `Sec-Fetch-Site` decides: `same-origin` and `none` pass, `same-site` and `cross-site` are refused;
3. browsers without that header send `Origin` on every POST: its host has to equal the `Host` the request was sent to;
4. with neither header (scripts, curl, tests) the request passes: only a browser can be tricked into a cross-site request.

Behind the Vite dev proxy the page and the API are the same origin for the browser (`Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`), so nothing needs configuring.

## Security headers

`configureSecurityHeaders` (`Application.kt`) sets the same headers on every response of the Ktor server: API answers, errors (`401`, `403`, `404`, `413`), CORS preflights and the files of the SPA. `SecurityHeadersTest` checks each of those kinds. `e2e/security-headers.spec.ts` reads the headers from whatever `BASE_URL` serves: the Vite dev server, which sets a development version of them in `frontend/vite.config.ts` (its `script-src` allows inline scripts for hot reloading), or the built image in the `e2e-image` job. A change to a header therefore goes into those three places.

- `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'; img-src 'self' data: blob:; connect-src 'self'; object-src 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'self'; form-action 'self'`. A page talks to its own origin and to nobody else: the weather is typed by hand (decision D12) and nothing calls Open-Meteo, so it is not listed.
- `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`.
- `Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=()`: features the application does not use are off for the page and for anything it embeds. `camera=()` governs `getUserMedia` only; the photo field is a plain `<input type="file">`, which hands the choice to the operating system and still opens the camera app on a phone. A feature that used `getUserMedia` would have to change this header.
- `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin`: no other site's window shares the browsing context group of the application.
- `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-site`, **not** `same-origin`: `same-origin` would stop photos from loading on an SPA host listed in `CORS_ALLOWED_ORIGINS`.
- `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains; preload`, in production only (plain HTTP development must not pin HTTPS).

## The screens: phone first, tokens and shared components (backlog B3)

The people who write the diary stand on a site with a phone in one hand, so a page is judged on a phone first: **360 px upright and 780 x 360 sideways**.

- **Touch rules follow the pointer, not only the width.** A phone held sideways is wider than the 640 px of Tailwind's `sm`, and still has a thumb. So for a **coarse pointer at any width** (and for any width under 640 px) text in a field is at least **16 px** (iOS zooms into a smaller field and does not zoom back; `index.css` sets it for every field, so the pages not yet converted are covered too), and for a coarse pointer every button and every one-line field is at least **44 px** high and a tick box is 24 px. The shared components do the same with Tailwind's `pointer-fine:` variant (`sm:pointer-fine:min-h-10`: only a mouse on a wide screen gets the lower, denser control). What a mouse has on a wide screen is 36 to 40 px, as before. Checked in `e2e/mobile-pages.spec.ts` on every main page, in both phone projects (`mobile`, `mobile-landscape`).
- **No page scrolls sideways** (a page wider than the screen is shrunk by the browser and its right side, the logout button, is off the screen: that was the state before). A word without a place to break wraps (`overflow-wrap: anywhere` on `body`); the spec's data has words of 63 to 120 letters with no hyphen or space, and fails when that rule is taken out. **The header is a grid**: logout stays top right at every width; one row from 1024 px, two between 640 and 1023 px, three below.
- **A table that has to be read on a phone** gets `max-sm:table-cards` (members, audit log) or, for a table too wide for a tablet (the users: about 1250 px), `max-xl:table-cards` (`index.css`, a Tailwind utility): its rows become blocks, a cell with a `data-label` starts with that caption, the header row stays for screen readers only, buttons and links in a cell are 44 px high. Above the breakpoint it is the same table and, if it must, it scrolls inside its box (`overflow-x-auto`), so nothing is cut off. **Known limit:** Safari with VoiceOver may stop announcing a table whose cells are `display: block` as a table. The project list is a list of cards that are links as a whole.
- **Tokens (`@theme` in `frontend/src/index.css`):** `page`, `surface`, `ink`, `ink-muted`, `line` (decoration, and the default border colour: Tailwind 4's bare `border` is `currentColor`), `input` (the boundary of a field or a button, at least 3:1), `focus`, and the tones `primary`, `danger`, `success`, `warning`, `info`, `neutral` with `-hover`, `-soft` (a tinted background) and `-ink` (text on it and on the surface). Every colour is a hex literal so that `lib/colorTokens.test.ts` can read it. **What that test guarantees:** every pair in the token list (body, muted, link, error, tones on their tint) is at least **4.5:1**, boundaries and the focus ring at least **3:1** (WCAG 2.2 AA); every `.ts` and `.tsx` file of `src/components` and `src/pages` is scanned, and a colour utility whose name is neither a token nor a Tailwind keyword (`bg-primry`) fails; and the foreground and background colours that really stand together in one class string (per state: plain, `hover:`, `disabled:`, `aria-invalid:`, `placeholder:`) and every border in one of our colours are derived from the source and checked. **What it does not see:** a class built at run time, a palette colour (`text-gray-500`) next to a token, the pixels. The three the review found too weak are replaced: input borders (gray-300, 1.5:1), white on emerald-600 (3.8:1) and red-500 text (3.8:1). **Transitional:** `--color-gray-300` is redefined to the accessible border colour, because the pages not yet converted still spell `border-gray-300`; delete it (and its check in the test) with the last page that does. There is **no dark mode**: `color-scheme: only light` in `index.css` and in a `<meta name="color-scheme" content="only light">` in `index.html` (a browser's forced dark mode would invert the colours). Dates and numbers use `tabular-nums`. `.prettierrc` has `tailwindStylesheet`, so the class sorter knows the tokens.
- **Shared components (`frontend/src/components/ui/`, import from `components/ui`):** every one builds its classes with `cn()` (tailwind-merge): **a `className` given to a component wins over the one it brings** (`className="w-auto"` replaces `w-full`; before, both stayed and the stylesheet decided), and the rest is kept (`cn.test.ts`). `Button` (primary, secondary, danger, danger-quiet, quiet; `buttonClasses` gives a link the same look), `Field` (the label tied to the control with `htmlFor`/`id`, the hint and the error with `aria-describedby`, the error `role="alert"` or, with `errorRole="status"`, polite; `TextInput`, `TextArea` and `Select` (the native `<select>`) take their `id`, `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid` from it), `Card`, `StatusBadge` (tones, filled or outlined), `Banner` (an error is `role="alert"`, the others `role="status"`), `PageState` (loading, error, empty) and `TabNav`/`TabLink` (the project's tabs: a 2 x 2 grid on a phone, the underlined row from `sm`; the current one is bold and underlined on a phone, and has a line under it from `sm`, so it is not told apart by colour alone).
- **`Modal` (the native `<dialog>`):** focus stays inside, the page behind is inert and **does not scroll** (`html.modal-open`, counted for nested dialogs), it scrolls inside on a small screen, and `aria-describedby` points to a short `description` only (a form is not one long description). **Escape never closes the dialog by itself**: the browser's closing is cancelled and the dialog decides. While `dismissible` is false (saving, a one-time password not written down yet) Escape does nothing; `onCancel` returning `false` refuses too; otherwise it closes before React takes it out of the document (so the focus can return to the opener) and the page stops rendering it. ## Database roles

The protections the migrations put into the database (the audit chain's triggers, the signed-records triggers, the append-only tables) are only as strong as the database role that the running application connects as. **A role that owns the tables can switch every one of those triggers off**, so anybody who stole the application's password could rewrite history and hide it. Therefore, in production, there are two roles (decision D5):

- the **owner** creates and changes the schema (`migrate`); its password is never on the application's machine;
- the **application's role** (`app`, created by `scripts/sql/bootstrap-app-role.sql`) owns nothing. `migrate` gives it, in `db/grants/app-role.sql`, data access to the tables (select, insert, update, delete; not truncate, not trigger) and read access to the schema history, and takes **update, delete and truncate on the audit log away**. It cannot create or drop a table, alter or disable a trigger, or change a role.

The application in production **does not migrate** (`MIGRATE_ON_START` defaults to false there): on a schema that is not current it refuses to start with "the database schema is out of date ... run the migrate command". The `create-admin` / `reset-password` / `set-ckait` commands follow the same rule in production. In development and in the tests one role does everything, as before.

```bash
# once per database, as a superuser (creates the role "app"):
psql -v ON_ERROR_STOP=1 -v app_password="$APP_DB_PASSWORD" -d stavebni_denik -f scripts/sql/bootstrap-app-role.sql
# at every release, with the OWNER's credentials, from CI or the operator's machine (not from the application's machine):
DB_MIGRATE_USER=<owner> DB_MIGRATE_PASSWORD=<...> DB_APP_ROLE=app JDBC_URL=<...> \
  java -cp backend-all.jar cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt migrate
```

`DatabaseRolesTest` runs the facts: under the application's role (a) normal work succeeds, (b) about twenty statements that would weaken the protections are refused (create, drop or alter a table, disable or drop a trigger or function, truncate, delete or update the audit log, change the schema history, alter or create a role), (c) the whole application flow (the service path: project, entry, signature, audit chain) works and the chain verifies, and (d) a table that a later migration adds is covered with the same limits. The CI Docker job does it for real: it creates the role, shows that an unmigrated schema stops the application, migrates as the owner, boots the image as the application's role, and checks that this role cannot create a table, disable a trigger, truncate or delete the audit log. **The simple one-role setup still works** (`MIGRATE_ON_START=true`, `DB_USER` = the owner), but then the application's role owns the tables and none of the above holds: use it for staging with test data only. Backups and the restore drill are in "Backups and the restore drill".

## Backups and the restore drill

The evidence is the database (including `audit_log`) **and** the photo files; the database holds a SHA-256 of each photo, so a database restored without its photos is detectably incomplete. `scripts/backup/` holds three scripts, all of them exercised in CI:

- `backup.sh` writes `denik-<UTC time>/`: `db.dump` (`pg_dump` custom format, one consistent snapshot, no owners or grants: whoever restores becomes the owner), `photos.tar.gz` (taken **after** the dump, so a photo that arrived in between is a harmless orphan file and never a database row without its file), `manifest.json` (schema version, number of photo files, **the newest audit-log row before the dump**) and `SHA256SUMS`. It prints that audit head: it is the anchor for the restored database (an anchor stays valid while the log grows) and belongs somewhere the database owner cannot reach (see "Audit log").
- `restore.sh` restores into an **empty** database and an empty uploads directory and refuses anything else, so a restore can never overwrite live data (to replace a damaged database, restore into a fresh one and switch over). It checks the backup's checksums, runs `pg_restore --exit-on-error`, (with `APP_JAR`) **migrates the restored copy to the schema of that jar** first, so a backup made by an older version can be verified by newer code (a backup of a newer schema cannot be migrated and fails there), checks **every photo file against the hash recorded at upload**, and, given `APP_JAR`, runs `audit-verify <anchor>` (the chain verifies and still contains the row the manifest recorded) and `verify-signatures` against the restored database. A backup of a chain restores to the same rows, to the byte, and verifies, anchors included (`AuditBackupRestoreTest`; see "Audit log"). It prints "restore drill passed" only when all of that holds.
- `drill.sh` does backup and restore and then shows that the restore **refuses** a non-empty target database, a damaged backup file, and a photo that was replaced even though the backup's own checksums were regenerated to match. A restore that cannot fail proves nothing.

`verify-signatures` (an operator command that only reads, like `audit-verify`) hashes every signed entry again from the database (text, signer, time, photos, and from format 3 the stored signer and part A snapshot: a missing one is a failure), every signature of a site handover protocol (the content of the protocol against the hash both parties signed, and each signature hash against its own columns; the audit rows of those signatures are not compared yet) and every photo file under `UPLOADS_DIR`, and compares them with what was recorded when the entry was signed and the photo uploaded; exit `1` names each entry that no longer matches. Entries signed before hashes existed are counted, not failed. **For an entry signed in format 3 it also reads the entry's `report.lock` audit row** (through its payload, the text the row's hash covers) and compares the five things that row records (the format, the signer's name and number, the SHA-256 of the part A snapshot and the signature hash) with the signed entry: a difference, a missing or unreadable row, and an entry whose row says format 3 but which was made to look older are failures that name the entry, the row and the fields (never what they hold). What that proves, together with `audit-verify` (the restore drill runs both): an owner of the table who switches the triggers off, edits the signer or part A and computes the signature hash again (the algorithm is public) no longer verifies, because the audit row is what they did not change, and could not change without breaking the chain from that row on, or the anchor if one was recorded after it. **What it cannot prove:** that a _compromised application_, which holds the application's database role and the audit lock, did not write a consistent signed row **and** a consistent `report.lock` row, in the one transaction as the application does, before the first anchor that covers them; nor that `signerName` is the name of the user row of the signer (see "What a signature covers"). After a restore this is what shows that **what was signed is as it was signed, as far as the chain and its anchors reach**.

CI runs `drill.sh` after the end-to-end tests, on the database and photos those tests left behind (real signed entries, addenda, remarks, acknowledgements, photos), so the drill is a check on every change, not a promise. **Not done, because it needs infrastructure that does not exist yet:** running `backup.sh` on a schedule, and copying the result to encrypted storage that the application's machine cannot delete from (the backup holds personal data and the evidence itself); and a periodic restore drill against production data. `PG_DOCKER_IMAGE` lets the scripts run `pg_dump`/`psql` from a container when the local client is older than the server (`pg_dump` refuses a newer server).

## Audit log

Every change goes through `AuditService`: the audit row and the business change share one transaction, appenders are serialised by an advisory lock, and each row carries the hash of the previous one (a chain). The database refuses `UPDATE`, `DELETE` (migration V1) and `TRUNCATE` (V2) on `audit_log` with triggers. Those triggers stop mistakes and an application that holds ordinary privileges; **whoever owns the table can still switch them off**, and a log that was cut short, or emptied, is still a valid chain. So the newest row has to be **recorded outside the database**, and checked against:

```bash
fly ssh console -C "java -cp /app/app.jar cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt audit-head"          # prints <id>:<hash>; store it somewhere the database owner cannot reach
fly ssh console -C "java -cp /app/app.jar cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt audit-verify 1234:ab12…"   # chain + that row 1234 still exists unchanged
```

- **Rows of users:**
  - Every change of a user (`user.create`, `user.update`, `user.activate`, `user.deactivate`, `user.delete`, `user.password_change`, `user.password_reset`, and the operator commands `user.bootstrap_admin` and `user.password_reset`) is written by `AuditService.auditedWrite`, after the change and in the same transaction: a change that fails leaves no row, and a row that cannot be written leaves no change. It is the only way an audit row is written (the legacy `auditedTransaction`, which wrote the row first, with `before = after = null` and the nickname as `entityId`, is gone).
  - `entityId` is the user's id. `before` and `after` are a `UserSnapshot` (`UserSnapshot.kt`): id, nickname, display name, role, administrator flag, active, `deletedAt`, ČKAIT number, `mustChangePwd`, `passwordChangedAt` and `passwordExpiresAt`. A creation has no `before`; a command-line action has no acting user. So `user.create` records the role, the administrator flag and the ČKAIT number the account started with.
  - **Never a password, a hash or a token:** the state of the password is only those flags and times (a temporary password has an expiry and no "chosen" time, a chosen one the opposite). `UserAuditRowsTest` checks the field names of the snapshot and searches every stored row for the passwords and hashes the operations produced.
- **Row format (hash version 2, backlog C3):**
  - **One format, one chain.** Every row is hashed the same way (`audit_log.hash_version` is 2: the database refuses any other number, and `verifyChain` reports one that got in anyway). The application has run nowhere but in tests and in CI, so no row of an earlier format exists and none is supported. The number is in the hash so that a **later** format can be told apart, and a row that was written is never hashed again (no migration touches a hash), so every anchor ever recorded stays valid. A row's `prev_hash` is the hash of the row before it: one global chain. An anchor, `audit-head`, `audit-verify`, `GET /api/audit/verify`, the anchor script and the manifest of a backup only handle `<id>:<row_hash>`.
  - **The byte layout** (the code is `AuditHash.kt`; `lp(x)` is the length of `x` as four bytes, big-endian, followed by `x`; both hashes are written as 64 lower-case hex characters). A verifier in another language is written from this block:

    ```
    payload_digest = SHA-256(
        00 00 00 1c  "stavebni-denik/audit-payload"   the tag, 28 bytes
        00 00 00 20  <32 bytes>                       the salt DECODED: payload_salt is these bytes as 64 hex characters, never hash the hex text
        LL LL LL LL  <payload_text, UTF-8>            its length in bytes, then the text exactly as it is stored
    )
    row_hash = SHA-256(
        00 00 00 18  "stavebni-denik/audit-row"       the tag, 24 bytes
        00 00 00 01  "2"                              the format
        00 00 00 40  <prev_hash>                      64 ASCII characters: the row hash of the row before (64 zeros for the first row)
        00 00 00 24  <project_id>                     36 ASCII characters, a lower-case UUID,
          or 00 00 00 00                              ... or, for a row of no project, a field of length ZERO (not an omitted field)
        00 00 00 40  <payload_digest>                 64 ASCII characters
    )
    ```

  - **The payload** (`payload_text`) is **the text of everything the row says**: a compact JSON object with the keys `action`, `actorId`, `after`, `before`, `entityId`, `entityType` and `ts` (UTC, milliseconds), keys sorted, numbers as the snapshot had them. It is stored as that text and **hashed as it is stored**, so verification never composes or re-parses anything in order to hash (backlog M10: `jsonb` rewrites numbers). **Read it as text:** parse `payload_text` as JSON in the reader (any JSON library; in `psql` `payload_text::json -> 'after'` keeps the text as written). **Not `::jsonb`**: it turns `1e-7` into `0.0000001` and refuses a `\u0000`. The salt (`payload_salt`) is 32 random bytes, **new for every row**. A payload nests arrays and objects at most **64** levels (its own object is the first): the writer refuses more, and `verifyChain` reports a deeper payload as a failed verification of that row (the depth is counted over the text, not by parsing it, so that a hostile row inserted past the application cannot end the verifier with a `StackOverflowError`; the command line answers `1` and names the row).
  - **A reference verifier**, standard library only, is `scripts/audit/verify-audit-hash.py` (it recomputes the golden vectors in `backend/src/test/resources/audit-hash-v2-vectors.json`, which `AuditHashTest` reads too, and verifies a chain of rows in full or as stubs: `python3 scripts/audit/verify-audit-hash.py vectors <file>` or `chain <rows.json>`). A test runs it, and another checks that the block below is the one in the script, so this text cannot drift from the code:

    <!-- audit-hash-spec:begin -->

    ```python
    import hashlib
    import struct

    GENESIS = "0" * 64


    def lp(data):
        """A field: its length as 4 bytes, big-endian, then the bytes."""
        return struct.pack(">I", len(data)) + data


    def payload_digest(salt_hex, payload_text):
        # The salt is the 32 DECODED bytes, not the 64 characters of hex text.
        return hashlib.sha256(
            lp(b"stavebni-denik/audit-payload") + lp(bytes.fromhex(salt_hex)) + lp(payload_text.encode("utf-8"))
        ).hexdigest()


    def row_hash(prev_hash, project_id, digest):
        # No project is a field of length zero (00 00 00 00), not an omitted field.
        project = (project_id or "").encode("ascii")
        return hashlib.sha256(
            lp(b"stavebni-denik/audit-row") + lp(b"2") + lp(prev_hash.encode("ascii")) + lp(project) + lp(digest.encode("ascii"))
        ).hexdigest()


    def verify_chain(rows):
        """rows: dicts with prev_hash, row_hash, project_id (or None) and either payload_digest (a stub) or
        payload_text and payload_salt (a row in full). Returns None when every hash and every link holds, otherwise the
        position of the first row that does not fit."""
        prev = GENESIS
        for position, row in enumerate(rows):
            digest = row.get("payload_digest") or payload_digest(row["payload_salt"], row["payload_text"])
            if row["prev_hash"] != prev or row_hash(row["prev_hash"], row.get("project_id"), digest) != row["row_hash"]:
                return position
            prev = row["row_hash"]
        return None
    ```

    <!-- audit-hash-spec:end -->

  - **What is stored** (the table is defined in `V1`): `hash_version`, `project_id` (nullable; no foreign key, because the evidence outlives anything), `payload_text` and `payload_salt`, with `id`, `prev_hash` and `row_hash`. `ts`, `actor_id`, `action`, `entity_type` and `entity_id` repeat the payload so that it can be searched; `verifyChain` checks that each says what the payload says. **There is no other column**: the old `before`, `after`, `ip` and `user_agent` are gone, so nothing readable sits in a row without being under its hash. Not covered: the row's `id` (the order is the chain's) and `audit_request_context` (decision D7). The append-only triggers (V1, V2) and the application role's grants are table level and cover the columns unchanged.
  - **Which project a row belongs to** (`AuditProject`, worked out inside `AuditService.auditedWrite` from what the change already says, so that no caller can forget it): `user.*` rows (also the operator commands) belong to none, because users are global; `project.*` rows (creation, identification, site manager, members) to the project itself; `report.*` (entry, signature, acknowledgements, addenda, entries of other parties), `photo.*`, `siteHandover.*` and `authorizedPerson.*` to the `projectId` of their snapshot. **Every action starts with its entity type** (`report.lock` is of the type `report`), checked when a row is written and when the chain is verified: the writer chooses the type and a user's row has no project, so otherwise a row about project A that said it was a user's would be a stub in every export. A row of a project that names none, and an entity type without a rule, are refused: the change is rolled back, the client gets a plain `500` with a Czech sentence and nothing internal, and the log says what was wrong (never the content of the row). **The rule is applied again when the chain is verified**, from the stored payload, by the same function: a row that names another project than its payload says (a stolen application password, or a bug, that labelled a row of A as B's or as nobody's) has right hashes and is found. So the rule is part of the format: it may grow (a new entity type, with a test: `AuditProjectRowsTest` runs every change of the application and fails when an audit action of the source has not been run, or a row names the wrong project), never change what a type means.
  - **One project's part of the chain (for the § 166(3) export, backlog C5):** a row hash is a function of three things only: the previous hash, the project and the salted digest of the payload. So the rows of one project can be handed over in full (`payload_text` and `payload_salt`) and every other row as a **stub**: `id`, `prev_hash`, `project_id`, `payload_digest`, `row_hash`. The recipient recomputes every hash and every link and checks that no stub names the project of the export. A row of that project hidden as a stub fails, because its project id is inside the hash. **What a stub shows, honestly:** that the row exists, where it sits in the chain, the id of its project, and (as ids repeat) how many rows each other project has. A project's id is a random UUID (`projects.id` defaults to `gen_random_uuid()`, unlike the time-ordered ids of the other tables), so it does not tell when the project was made. It does **not** show what the row says: the time, actor, action and entity of the row are under the digest, the digest is salted and the salt is never in a stub, so a recipient cannot confirm a guess about a row of another project (an unsalted digest would let them test the guess). `AuditChainVerificationTest` does exactly this with the export of one project out of three.
  - **Limits:** a cut tail is not seen by a slice (as for the chain itself, see the anchor below); the exporter must never write the `payload_salt` of a row of another project into an export; the rows of users are stubs in every export. `verifyChain` reads the chain in pieces of at most 1000 rows **and** about 8 MiB of payload (a payload can hold a whole entry with long texts and a request may be 256 KiB: one piece of 1000 such rows would need 256 MB of heap on a machine with 400 MB), asking for the size first and then reading only that many rows.
- **Verification commands and API:**
  - `audit-verify` exits `0` when the chain is intact (and the anchor, if given, is found unchanged) and `1` when it is not, printing the reason; `3` means the check **could not run** (database unreachable, schema missing): that is neither "intact" nor "broken", and an unattended check must treat it as a failure.
  - All three verification commands (`audit-head`, `audit-verify`, and `verify-signatures`) only read: they **do not migrate** the schema, so a read-only database role is enough and a scheduler cannot change a database that another version of the application owns. Without an anchor the check cannot see a cut tail.
  - The same check is available to administrators as `GET /api/audit/verify` and `GET /api/audit/verify?anchor=<id>:<hash>`, which also returns the current `head`. An anchor stays valid while the log grows.
- **Nightly external anchor (decision D4, `.github/workflows/audit-anchor.yml` + `scripts/audit/record-anchor.sh`):**
  1. Checks that the newest record in a PRIVATE anchor repository still matches its RFC 3161 timestamp token.
  2. Verifies the whole chain against the head that record names (intact and not cut below it) and takes the new head **from that same run** (a second run could be shown a different log).
  3. Writes the new head as `anchors/anchor-<UTC time>.txt` (head, time, the previous record and its SHA-256) with the token request and the token from an independent time-stamping authority, verifies that token against the authority's certificate **pinned in the anchor repository** (`tsa/ca.pem`), and pushes it.
  - Nothing is recorded when any check fails (exit `1` chain or record not intact, `2` not configured, `3` could not complete), and the push is never forced, so it fails when the anchor repository moved during the run.
  - A history rewritten earlier is **not** detected by the push: the branch rule (no force-push, no deletion) and the time-stamped, hash-linked records are what protect it, and a record that was edited no longer matches its token. A token is verified as of the time it was issued, so a rotated authority certificate does not fail old records (add the new certificate to `tsa/ca.pem`).
  - **Limits to know:** what is written after the last record (up to a day, longer when a night fails) is not protected yet; the job cannot tell you that it stopped running (a failed night notifies, but GitHub also switches scheduled workflows off in a repository without activity for 60 days, silently): watch the age of the newest record with something outside this workflow.
  - **Testing & verification:** Tested in CI (`scripts/audit/anchor-test.sh`) against a real database and a real RFC 3161 exchange with a throwaway authority, including the refusals: an unreachable authority, an earlier record edited after it was time-stamped, a log that lacks the row an earlier record named, and a certificate that is not the pinned one. What a verifier does later: `openssl ts -verify -data anchors/anchor-….txt -in anchors/anchor-….tsr -CAfile tsa/ca.pem` proves the record existed at that time, and `audit-verify <head>` proves the database still contains that row unchanged.
  - **Still to do by a person:** create the private anchor repository and its branch rule, pin the authority's certificate, and set the secrets (`AUDIT_JDBC_URL`, `AUDIT_DB_USER`, `AUDIT_DB_PASSWORD`, `AUDIT_ANCHOR_TOKEN`) and variables (`AUDIT_ANCHOR_REPO`, optional `AUDIT_TSA_URL`) once a production database exists (`docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`). (Printing the entry's `report.lock` row in the PDF footer is code that is not written yet; backlog B8.) **The workflow fails while it is not configured**, so that nobody believes the log is watched when it is not (disable it until a production database exists). The database must be reachable from GitHub's runners; otherwise run the same script where the database is. Separate database roles for migrations and for the application (decision D5) are done (see "Database roles"); what is still to do is to use them in the real production database.

- **Address and user agent (decision D7):**
  - The IP address and user agent of a request are personal data, so they are **not part of the audit hash**: the chain is permanent and cannot be pruned. `AuditService` writes them, in the same transaction as the audit row, to `audit_request_context` (one row per audit row; they come from the `RequestContext` the server puts on every call, so a change made by an operator command has none; the user agent is cut to 256 characters).
  - Sign-ins, failed sign-ins and sign-outs go to `access_log`, which is **not** part of the legal chain: it is a record for security investigations. A failed attempt against a name that does not exist records no name at all, because what was typed into that field may be a password; against an existing account it records the account. Not recorded: a failed attempt that the rate limiter refused (it is already counted), and anything about reads.
  - **12-month retention:** Both tables are kept **twelve months**: the database refuses to change a row, or to delete one younger than that (migration V12; whoever owns the table can still switch the trigger off, as with the others), so that someone holding the application's credentials cannot erase their traces, and `AdminCliKt prune-access-records` deletes what is older. **Run it regularly** (`docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`): the period is a promise made in the privacy notice. Backups contain these tables too, so they follow the same period.
  - **Worker names in the permanent chain:** The audit snapshots of an entry contain what the entry contains, including the **names of the people on site** (the law asks for them in the diary), and the audit chain is permanent and cannot be pruned: those names cannot be erased from it. Their legal basis is the duty to keep the diary; it belongs in the GDPR paperwork (decision D13). The same holds, since signature format 3, for the **signer's name and ČKAIT number** (the `report.lock` row holds them) and, in the signed entry itself (not in the chain, which holds only the digest of it), for the names, ČKAIT numbers and scopes of the persons of part A (`partASnapshot`): a signed entry is never changed, so they cannot be corrected or erased there either. The same holds for the **signer's name and, for the contractor, ČKAIT number** in the `siteHandover.sign` rows of a handover protocol (and for the participants, notes and meters of any protocol, which are in its snapshots). And the **text of a member's scope** is in the `project.member.add` audit rows (before and after, as a text), so it too stays in the permanent chain: a scope should say what a person ensures and answers for on the build, and not more.

## First administrator and operator CLI (`AdminCliKt`)

A fresh database has no users, so nobody can log in. Operator commands run against the database from inside the application image (`cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt`). Except `migrate`, they connect as the application's role (`JDBC_URL`, `DB_USER`, `DB_PASSWORD`). **In production** no command but `migrate` changes the schema, and each of them refuses to run on a schema that is not current (exit `3`). Elsewhere `create-admin`, `reset-password`, `set-ckait` and `prune-access-records` migrate the schema first; the three read-only commands never do.

- `migrate`: runs the Flyway migrations as the schema owner (`DB_MIGRATE_USER`, `DB_MIGRATE_PASSWORD`, falling back to `DB_USER`/`DB_PASSWORD`) and applies `db/grants/app-role.sql` when `DB_APP_ROLE` is set; `1` when the database has migrations this build does not know.
- `create-admin <nickname> <displayName>`: creates the first administrator (refused once an active administrator exists).
- `reset-password <nickname>`: issues a new temporary password, ends all sessions of the user and lifts a login lockout.
- `set-ckait <nickname> <number>`: sets or replaces the ČKAIT number of an account (not deleted; a deactivated one too). An administrator cannot change their own number in the application, so on a fresh installation whose only administrator also has to sign, this is how the first number gets in. It ends no session, needs and touches no password, and writes one `user.update` audit row without an acting user, with the account (and so the number) as it was and as it became; the same number again writes nothing. Refused (`1`) for an unknown or deleted user, a blank number or one over 50 characters.
- `audit-head`: prints the newest `audit_log` row as `<id>:<hash>` (read-only; `1` when the chain is broken or the log is empty).
- `audit-verify [<id>:<hash>]`: verifies the hash chain and the optional anchor (read-only; `0` intact, `1` broken).
- `verify-signatures`: hashes every signed entry again, every signature of a site handover protocol (its content and its signature hash) and every photo file under `UPLOADS_DIR`, and compares them with what was recorded (read-only; `0` intact, `1` mismatch).
- `prune-access-records`: deletes the rows of `access_log` and `audit_request_context` older than 12 months, and the rows of `rate_limit_attempts` older than 1 day (they only count inside a window of 15 minutes at most; the limiter cleans up a key only when it sees it again, so those of addresses and names that never return stayed; no trigger guards that table and the application's role may delete from it).

Every command exits `2` on wrong usage and `3` when it **could not run** (database unreachable, schema missing or not current): `3` is neither "intact" nor "broken", and an unattended check must treat it as a failure.

```bash
# first administrator (refused as soon as an active administrator exists)
fly ssh console -C "java -cp /app/app.jar cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt create-admin alice 'Alice Novakova'"

# the only administrator is locked out or forgot the password: new temporary password, all sessions end, a login lockout is lifted
fly ssh console -C "java -cp /app/app.jar cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt reset-password alice"
```

Both `create-admin` and `reset-password` print a generated password once, to standard output only (framework logging is reduced to warnings and errors, which share the terminal); it is stored only as an Argon2id hash, expires after 7 days, the account has to change it at the first login, and the audit log records the action (`user.bootstrap_admin`, `user.password_reset`, with the user's id and the account as it was and as it became) without an acting user and without the password. `create-admin` makes the account a project manager (BOSS) with the administrator flag; further users are created in the application. Exit codes: `0` done, `1` refused (for example an administrator already exists), `2` wrong usage, `3` the command could not be carried out (database unreachable, schema not current in production). Locally: `java -cp backend/build/libs/backend-all.jar cz.stavebni.denik.cli.AdminCliKt …` after `./gradlew :backend:shadowJar`.

## Follow-ups

- **Materials, visits and notifications do not exist.** The unreachable `VisitService`, `MaterialService` and `NotificationService` (no routes, no UI, no audit rows or none of the in-transaction locks) and the permissions of the first two were removed, together with the three pieces of the SPA that showed invented data (a dashboard with "Project A" and "Project B", a notification bell with fixed messages, a photo gallery with placeholder photos), none of which was connected to the backend; the tables (`material_needs`, `visits`, `notifications`) are still in the schema. Decision D6 puts them after go-live; when they come back they are written on the audited path (`AuditService.auditedWrite`, project role, locks) from the start, not revived from the old code.
- **A ČKAIT number is not verified, and the rule about the own number is a speed bump (decision D2, backlog A1).** The application checks that a number is entered, not that it is real. An administrator cannot change their own number, but can still qualify themselves in a second, visible step: mint a second administrator with a number (`createUser` takes `isAdmin` and `ckaitNumber`) and use that account, reset the password of another administrator, or add a manager with an invented number through the audited way in (decision D1). Everything is recorded (`user.create`, `user.update`, `user.password_reset`, `project.member.add`), so it can be seen afterwards; none of it is prevented. A two-person rule (a second administrator confirms a number) or a check of the declared number against the chamber's register would close it: a decision of the owner and a later item. `set-ckait` rests on the operator's access to the database in the same way.
- **Photos cannot be removed.** `PhotoService.deletePhoto` exists and the matrix allows it to BOSS, but no route calls it, so a photo uploaded to the wrong entry stays there and becomes part of what is signed.
- **Upload limits, what is left:** a person who may upload and sends a byte a minute holds a body place until the 10-minute limit ends it (2 per user, 8 in all, and only people with the right to upload get one, so a few members of a project who agree on it can block other people's uploads for that long); the gate counts per process, which is right for one machine and would need a shared count for several; the numbers are fixed in code (`UploadLimits`, `RateLimiter.Rules`) and not configurable; a progressive JPEG within the limits still takes up to about 100 MiB of native memory and 5 s of CPU per decode, and the header walk is a copy of how libjpeg reads markers: the in-decoder bound is what holds if the two ever differ, and it stops a decode only at the next pass or scanline (not inside a single slow call); the heap arithmetic for bodies and images is an estimate, not a load test; `BoundedRequestBody` is a copy of a Ktor plugin and should be dropped when Ktor flushes its own (check after each Ktor upgrade: `BoundedRequestBodyTest` pins the behaviour that is needed).
- **The PDF of an entry** does not print the handover protocols, or the ČKAIT number next to entries of other parties and acknowledgements (backlog B9; the persons with a role and the authorised persons are printed in part A since backlog C2, and part A and the signer are those of the day of signing from signature format 3). The photos are listed with the hash of each file, not shown: the PDF has no images. **What signature format 3 does not freeze** (a later format could, at the price of a format 4): the names of the author of the entry, of the people who uploaded photos and of those named under acknowledgements, addenda and entries of other parties are read from the accounts as they are now, so renaming such a user changes what an earlier PDF prints for them (the audit rows of the user, `user.create` and `user.update`, record each name as it was); and the weather is signed as the text `WeatherData.describe()` makes of it, which is frozen with the format.
- **The web application on the site:** the phone shell is done (backlog B3, see "The screens": header, tabs and tables fit 360 px; the form of an entry and the overlays are not yet reshaped for a phone), and so are the entry as a document and the lists (backlog B4). Only the daily entry keeps a draft; the other forms (new project, the user dialogs) lose unsaved text when the tab dies, and the project's identification form only asks before it is left. The form of an entry that is being written is still the old one (labels not tied to their fields, no previous and next day, no weather chips: B5 to B7); a signed entry and the entry of a reader are documents since backlog B4. The full list and the order of the work are in [`docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md`](docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md) (backlog, track B).
- **Code without a route, and leftovers:** `DashboardService`, `CsvExportService`, `ValidationService` and `WeatherService` are used only by their tests. The Vite starter files (`frontend/src/App.tsx`, `App.css`, `src/assets/*`, `public/icons.svg`) are not used. `ktor-server-rate-limit`, `ktor-server-sessions`, `ktor-server-call-logging` and `konform` are unused dependencies.
- **Backups are not scheduled and not copied off the machine.** The scripts and the restore drill exist and run in CI (see "Backups and the restore drill"); what is missing is a schedule, an encrypted off-site copy that the application's machine cannot delete from, and a periodic drill against production data. All of it needs the production infrastructure.
- **No second factor.** Signing in rests on a name and a password alone; a leaked or watched-over password gives full access to the diary (and, for an administrator, to user management and the audit log). Rate limits, the password policy and the breached-password check lower the risk and do not remove it. Proposal, not decided: TOTP (RFC 6238) as a second step after the password, with a QR code and a manual key at activation, one-time backup codes stored only as hashes, its own rate limit on code entry, and the secret stored encrypted. Required first for administrators and project managers (BOSS), optional for the rest; a reset by an administrator, audited like a password reset. Its confirmation at signing (decision D8) is a separate question.
- **No monitoring or alerts yet.** Nothing watches uptime, errors, disk or the database; a failed nightly audit-anchor run is the only notification that exists. Needs the production infrastructure (item 13 of [`docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md`](docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md)).
- **Deprecation warnings from the dependency bumps** (not fixed): `Principal` in Ktor (`SessionUser.kt`, and the unused `UserPrincipal` in `Security.kt`), `PartData.FileItem.streamProvider` (becomes `provider()`, keeping the 5 MiB cap) and `dispose` (becomes `release()`) in `PhotoRoutes`, and a `Json {}` built on every call in `ReportRoutes` and `PdfExportService`.
- **Smaller password gaps:** the change-password form does not check breached passwords while typing (the server does, on submit).
- The review that led to this design, the plan with its decisions D1–D13 and the legal notes are in [`docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md`](docs/REVIEW-AND-PLAN.md); this file stays the source of truth for how the system behaves.
